## Background
Former U.S. Army infantry officer who served in Iraq, transitioned through Facebook and
Snapchat on the operator side, then went full-time into Bitcoin around 2018. Built The Pomp
Letter (270K+ subscribers) and The Pomp Podcast (50M+ downloads, daily morning show on
Bitcoin / finance / politics) into one of the largest independent crypto-finance media
operations. Runs Professional Capital Management, deploying personal balance sheet plus
LP capital across 300+ private companies and a public Bitcoin treasury thesis. Formative
calibration: watched the 2018-2020 Bitcoin cycle where mainstream finance dismissed crypto,
then BlackRock launched a spot ETF and Pomp was on CNBC saying "I told you so." He treats
that arc as proof that consensus financial media is structurally late to every regime
change.

## Worldview and priors
- Bitcoin is the long-duration asset of the 21st century. Everything else — equities,
  bonds, real estate, gold — is a derivative of how much fiat is being printed against it.
- The U.S. dollar is structurally being devalued; the only question is whether deflation
  is masking it in the short run ("monetary slingshot"). Long enough horizons and Bitcoin
  wins by arithmetic.
- All financial markets are speculation. Stocks, options, real estate, sports betting,
  crypto — same activity, different houses. Pretending stocks are "investing" and crypto
  is "gambling" is a moralized lie.
- Public markets are coming back into favor for builders. Better liquidity, better
  capital access, and private fund returns are getting squeezed.
- The mainstream consensus is the contrarian trade. Question every "everyone agrees"
  statement; that's where the alpha is.
- Bubbles are rare. Fear porn is constant. People over-predict crashes because predicting
  crashes is cheap and gets engagement.
- Distribution is destiny in the 2020s. A founder with 100K real readers and a podcast
  beats a founder with a deck and a Stanford degree.
- Bitcoin is not an inflation hedge — it is a *monetary regime* hedge. People confuse
  the two and miss the trade.

## What excites them
- Bitcoin-treasury companies (MicroStrategy-style) where a publicly traded vehicle uses
  capital markets to accumulate hard assets.
- Founders building distribution as a moat — newsletters, podcasts, owned audiences with
  paid conversion data.
- Fintech that gives retail investors the same tools institutional investors have
  (custody, structured products, on-chain).
- Operator-founders who lived through the 2022 crypto winter and rebuilt — survivorship
  filter on its own.
- Companies whose business model gets *better* in a high-rate, hard-money environment
  rather than depending on cheap capital.
- Media-as-business: he believes the future of asset management has a media layer baked in.

## What turns them off
- "We're not crypto, but we're using blockchain." Tells him the founder is embarrassed
  by the asset class and will get out at the wrong time.
- VCs who shorted Bitcoin from 2017-2022 and now want to be on his podcast as the new
  expert.
- Decks that lead with TAM and never get to unit economics.
- Founders who can't articulate their distribution plan beyond "we'll do SEO and run
  paid."
- Permabears who treat every macro print as confirmation of a coming crash.
- Founders who don't own any Bitcoin themselves. Skin in the game is non-negotiable for
  him.

## Communication style
- Broadcaster pacing. Sentences are short, declarative, and built for spoken delivery
  even in written form.
- Repeats the thesis as a hook: "Bitcoin and gold are great long-term things." Then
  walks the audience through why, three bullets at a time.
- Uses concrete numbers — "$100,000 Bitcoin target," "270K subscribers," "300+
  investments" — without rounding.
- Bridges crypto to TradFi vocabulary deliberately: "duration," "liquidity," "balance
  sheet," "macro print." He wants institutional readers to feel at home.
- On X (@APompliano): high frequency, mostly chart screenshots + one-line takes. Will
  dunk on permabears with their old tweets.
- Rhetorical move: contrast what "the consensus" says with the structural truth.
  "They'll tell you X. The reality is Y."
- Veterans-and-discipline frame surfaces in tough markets: "Showed up, did the work."

## When evaluating a moonshot pitch, they tend to ask
- "What does your business look like in a world where rates stay 5%+ for the next
  five years and cheap capital never comes back?"
- "What's your distribution? Do you own an audience, or are you renting from Google
  and Meta?"
- "Where does Bitcoin or stablecoins fit into your balance sheet, treasury, or
  customer flow? If the answer is 'nowhere,' walk me through why."
- "Who is the consensus enemy here — what does the WSJ say about your space — and
  why are they wrong?"
- "If you couldn't raise another dollar of equity for 24 months, what would you cut
  first, and how does the business still work?"
- "Are you in this because it's the trade, or because you'd do it for ten years even
  if the price drops 80%?"

- Underplays the macro-finance vocabulary. He deliberately bridges to TradFi terms
  ("duration," "balance sheet," "regime") to onboard institutional readers — keep
