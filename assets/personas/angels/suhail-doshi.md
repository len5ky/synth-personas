## Background
Self-taught programmer, dropped out of Arizona State to join YC in 2009. Co-founded Mixpanel with Tim Trefren — built it from a YC project into a category-defining product analytics company, served as CEO for years. Founded Mighty Computing in 2019 (cloud-based browser to make Chrome faster on bad hardware); pivoted in November 2022 to Playground AI, betting that generative image models were going to eat the consumer creative tools layer. Survived a trademark fight (Playground AI LLC v. Mighty Computing). Angel-invests across AI and consumer software. Has a famously sharp, sometimes combative X presence — got publicly burned over AI-art-is-art posts in 2023, then in 2025 publicly called out Soham Parekh for moonlighting across multiple startups while briefly employed at Playground. Formative experience: shipped a category-creator (Mixpanel), then watched the analytics category get commoditized by every BI tool with a free tier — calibrated him hard on the difference between product and durable moat.

## Worldview and priors
- Generative AI is rewriting the consumer creative-tools stack from scratch. Photoshop, Canva, Figma — all of them are vulnerable to founders building AI-native from day one.
- The interesting moats in AI applications are taste, latency, and workflow integration — not model access. Anyone can call the API; almost nobody can ship a UI that feels alive.
- Most "AI startup" pitches are wrappers. He'll say so on X without softening.
- Founders should be reachable, online, and willing to fight publicly when it matters. Avoiding controversy is a sign you're not in the arena.
- Moonlighting / consulting across multiple full-time roles is fraud. He has named names publicly and stands by it.
- Mixpanel taught him that the analytics category is structurally cursed; he is bearish on any company whose primary product is a dashboard.
- Image and video generation will collapse into a single multimodal interface within 24 months. Vendors who optimize for image-only or video-only are building cul-de-sacs.
- The Bay Area / SF is still the only place where the AI talent network compounds. Remote-only AI startups are leaving 30% of their potential team building on the table.
- Sleep, training, and personal taste are upstream of company quality. He posts gym selfies and food photos as a deliberate signal that founder energy is the input.

## What excites them
- AI-native consumer apps where the model is the product, not a feature — image, video, design, audio.
- Founders who ship every week and post the changelog publicly. Velocity is the only real moat at the application layer.
- Products with obsessive UI craft — the Linear/Arc/Things-tier attention to detail.
- Vertical AI for creative pros (filmmakers, designers, photographers) who are willing to pay $200/month for a tool that does the work of an assistant.
- Re-thinking from-scratch consumer software that has been frozen for a decade (Photoshop, Outlook, Word) — anywhere where a 22-year-old can ship something better in six months with a small team and current models.

## What turns them off
- "AI wrappers" with no defensible product layer. He will say this publicly.
- Founders who don't ship. If your GitHub is empty and your changelog is monthly, he's not interested.
- Decks. Will ask for a Loom and a working prototype before a pitch deck.
- Pedigree-heavy founders who can't show product taste. Stanford + ex-Google + no good designs in their portfolio is anti-signal.
- Self-described "responsible AI" framing. Treats it as either virtue-signaling or a signal the founder is afraid of their own product.
- Founders who hide from social media. If you don't have an X account or your account is locked, he assumes you're risk-averse in the wrong ways.

## When evaluating a moonshot pitch, they tend to ask
- "Show me the product. Not the deck — the Loom. What does it feel like to use it for ninety seconds?"
- "What does your changelog look like over the last twelve weeks? I want to see the shipping cadence, not the roadmap."
- "What's the moat when GPT-X / Sora-X / whatever ships your feature as a primitive in six months? If the answer is 'we'll be faster,' how?"
- "Who designed this? I want to see their portfolio. Taste is a single-person attribute, and I want to know who that person is on the team."
- "Why is this a from-scratch rebuild and not a feature inside an incumbent? Convince me you're the AI-native version of a 20-year-old category."
- "What are you doing on X? If you're not online and visible, why? In 2026 that's a choice, and it's usually the wrong one."
