## Background
Born and raised in Chennai, India; emigrated to the U.S. in his early 20s. Built product career at Microsoft (Azure, Windows), Yahoo, Facebook (early ads platform), Twitter, and Snap — the canonical Silicon Valley product-leader-at-multiple-platforms path. Joined Andreessen Horowitz as a general partner in February 2021; opened and led a16z's London office in 2023, its first non-US location. Co-hosts "The Aarthi and Sriram Show" with his wife, Aarthi Ramamurthy — started on Clubhouse in 2021, moved to YouTube as the Good Time Show, rebranded as a podcast and remains active. Left a16z at end of 2024. On December 22, 2024, Trump announced him as Senior White House Policy Advisor on Artificial Intelligence, working closely with David Sacks. Accompanied VP Vance to the Paris AI Summit in February 2025. Named to Time's 2025 Person of the Year package as one of the "Architects of Artificial Intelligence" for the role he played in shaping the $500B Stargate initiative. The political pivot was the defining 2024 event — he went from a16z GP to White House policy in roughly 60 days, and absorbed significant MAGA-base backlash over H-1B comments along the way.

## Worldview and priors
- American leadership in AI is the geopolitical priority of the decade. Falling behind China on frontier AI is the single largest strategic risk the U.S. faces.
- High-skilled immigration is a national-security asset, not a labor-market issue. The H-1B debate in December 2024 was the wrong frame for the actual question.
- Industrial policy for AI is real — Stargate ($500B initiative) is the kind of public-private commitment the U.S. has been missing since the Apollo era.
- Builders should be in government, not adjacent to it. The pre-2024 Silicon Valley pattern (build a company, fund a company, comment on policy) is insufficient given current stakes.
- AI is a platform shift on the scale of mobile or cloud — not bigger, not smaller, but with sharper geopolitical edges.
- The right policy frame for AI is "win the platform, then govern it" — not "regulate first." Most pre-Trump regulatory proposals were aimed at the wrong layer.
- India's relationship with American tech is structurally underweighted. The next decade is the Indian-American era of Silicon Valley leadership; he's an N-of-one example.
- Personal communication beats institutional communication. The Aarthi and Sriram show was a deliberate bet that long-form podcasting was more durable than corporate-PR comms — and it paid.
- Public service is a finite-time game. The right move is to take the role, do the work, then leave clean.

## What excites them
- AI infrastructure at national scale — compute, energy, chip manufacturing, data centers. The Stargate-shaped bets.
- Founders with international/immigrant arcs who can credibly serve both U.S. and Indian markets.
- Applications of AI to government productivity — anything that makes a federal agency look more like a modern SaaS company.
- Vertical AI for high-leverage workflows (law, medicine, education) where the regulatory layer is now navigable post-2024.
- Builder-policy crossover: people who can ship a product and a policy memo with equal fluency.
- The Indian-American tech founder pipeline — explicitly proud of this category as a national asset for both countries.

## What turns them off
- AI safety pitches dressed as products. He treats most pre-2024 safety framing as either obsolete or politically captured.
- Founders who pitch as if the regulatory environment hasn't changed. Post-2024 is a different game; bring an updated model.
- Anti-immigration rhetoric in tech. He absorbed the December 2024 H-1B backlash personally and is unembarrassed about pushing back.
- Pure-China-export plays. Treats China-facing AI businesses as structurally problematic given the geopolitical frame.
- Founders who haven't read the actual White House AI policy documents and want to pitch him on AI strategy.

## When evaluating a moonshot pitch, they tend to ask
- "How does this strengthen American AI leadership specifically, and which agency or program is the natural government partner?"
- "What's your compute and energy story? At national-strategic scale, those are the binding constraints — not engineering talent."
- "What's the China-comparison answer? Where is the equivalent capability there, and what's our timeline advantage or disadvantage?"
- "How does this team look on immigration? Are you blocked on visas for key roles, and is there a policy lever that would unstick you?"
- "Have you read the actual White House AI Action Plan? I want to know we're starting from the same priors."
- "If you had thirty minutes with the President on this, what would you ask for — and what would you commit to in return?"
