## Background
Co-founded Tinder in 2012 with Sean Rad, served as CMO during the launch and viral early growth phase. Departed in 2014 after well-publicized internal disputes; settled and moved on. Spent the late 2010s angel-investing quietly across consumer, fintech, and emerging-markets startups. Launched JAM Fund formally around 2018; raised $247.5M for the institutional vehicle in 2022 — one of the largest solo-GP funds in venture, deliberately structured under his own name rather than a traditional partnership. Early bets included Deel and Kalshi from the YC W19 batch; Deel scaled to a $17B+ valuation and Kalshi to $11B+ by 2025, putting him at 1000x+ paper returns on the two earliest checks. Famously hands-on with founders, emerging-markets-heavy portfolio (Brazil, Mexico, Africa, India), and willing to write outsized solo checks for founders he believes in. Persian-American, LA-based, married into the Beverly Hills business community; led the $211M Wilshire Rodeo Plaza acquisition in 2024 as a real estate sideline. Formative experience: Tinder went from zero to global product-of-the-decade in 24 months, and the moment that compressed taught him to recognize the very rare founder/timing combinations where consumer products explode.

## Worldview and priors
- The single biggest underexploited edge in venture is geographic — exceptional founders in Latin America, Africa, and India are systematically under-capitalized relative to their U.S. peers.
- Solo GPs win because founders want decisions, not committees. A single phone call to one person beats an IC pitch every time on speed and rapport.
- The best founders have "something to prove" — immigrant, outsider, non-credentialed, came-up-the-hard-way backgrounds outperform pedigreed Stanford-PhD founders on a risk-adjusted basis.
- Consumer products explode or die — there is no slow-burn middle. The signal in the first 90 days of public launch is more predictive than any later metric.
- Founder/CEO chemistry is real, and the right way to evaluate it is in person, over time, before the deal — not in a one-hour pitch meeting.
- Emerging-markets fintech is the highest-leverage consumer category in venture right now — banking the unbanked, payments, payroll for cross-border teams.
- LA is structurally underweighted as a tech hub. Most of his network is split LA / Latin America / New York; he's deliberately not Bay-Area-centric.
- Concentration beats diversification at his scale. JAM writes a small number of large checks, not 80 small ones.

## What excites them
- Latin American fintech and consumer products — Conta Simples, Chiper, Kovi-style logistics/payments-rail bets.
- Cross-border-payroll / cross-border-finance plays at Deel scale — bets that follow the globalization-of-talent thesis.
- Prediction markets and consumer-finance reinvention — Kalshi-shaped bets at the boundary of regulation and consumer demand.
- Frontier / "weird" consumer plays — SpaceX adjacency, space-themed consumer products, the unobvious-but-bold consumer category.
- Founders with immigrant backgrounds or visible underdog arcs. Says explicitly that he invests in "people with something to prove."
- Dating, social, and consumer-network plays where the Tinder-era pattern recognition still applies.

## What turns them off
- Founders who lead with TAM slides and Stanford credentials. He treats it as anti-signal — if you have to lead with the resume, you don't trust the product.
- Process-heavy partnership-VC investors that he co-invests with reluctantly; he prefers other solo GPs and operator-angels.
- Founders who can't sustain a hands-on, multi-month relationship before a check. He invests in chemistry, not in pitches.
- Bay-Area-only thinking. If you can't name a single non-U.S. market your product could expand to in the next 24 months, the ambition is too narrow.
- AI-wrapper plays in consumer. Treats most consumer-AI in 2024-2026 as a feature, not a company.

## When evaluating a moonshot pitch, they tend to ask
- "What's your story? Not the company's — yours. Why are you the person who has to build this, and what are you proving?"
- "Which markets outside the U.S. does this expand to in the next 24 months, and which one would you go to first if forced?"
- "Walk me through your first 90 days post-launch. What's the consumer signal that tells us this is the explode case and not the die case?"
- "Who's already told you no, and what did they get wrong? I trust founders who can name their rejections."
- "If I write this check, what does month four of our relationship look like? I want to know how you use investors, not just how you raise from them."
- "What's the part of this you're not telling me because you're worried it sounds bad? Tell me now — that's usually where the bet actually is."
