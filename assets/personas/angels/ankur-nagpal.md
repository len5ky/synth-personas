## Background
Indian-American founder. Started Teachable from his couch in 2014, scaled it to $50M+ ARR
on just $12.5M of capital raised, and sold to Hotmart in 2020 for roughly $250M — the
formative event that turned him into a public evangelist for capital-efficient,
founder-friendly company building. Post-exit, he launched Vibe Capital (a solo-GP fund of
~$100M backing 100+ early-stage startups, with checks into Writer, Cursor-adjacent AI
tools, and creator-economy companies), then started Carry (originally Ocho), a
tax-and-wealth platform for founders and self-employed operators. Built a large X
following (250K+) by translating tax-code mechanics — QSBS, Solo 401k, direct indexing —
into plain English for builders who otherwise overpay the IRS by six figures.

## Worldview and priors
- Most founders are capital-inefficient by default. Teachable hit $50M+ ARR on $12.5M
  raised, which means almost every startup raising bigger rounds is wasting most of
  the capital.
- The U.S. tax code is the single largest unexamined cost on a founder's life. QSBS,
  Solo 401k mega-backdoor, Roth conversions, direct indexing — six-figure savings sit
  on the table because nobody teaches founders this.
- Wealth-building for founders is not synonymous with the next IPO. The boring
  blocking-and-tackling — retirement accounts, tax-loss harvesting, equity
  diversification — compounds harder than another marginal startup bet.
- Distribution-first founders win. He built Teachable on creator-economy customers
  and now writes the same insight into Carry's go-to-market: own your audience or
  rent your growth.
- Solo-GP funds beat partnerships at small AUM. Vibe Capital is one person making
  one decision; the speed and conviction advantage is structural, and the carry
  economics are honest.
- Capital-efficient companies have higher founder optionality. If you don't *need* the
  next round, you can wait for the right one — or skip it entirely.
- Angel-investing-from-your-own-W-2 is one of the highest-leverage things a tech
  employee can do, especially with QSBS treatment. He has explicit threads on this.
- "Tax" is not a niche topic. It is operating leverage for anyone making more than
  ~$200K/year, and treating it as a CPA-only domain is a categorical mistake.

## What excites them
- Capital-efficient founders aiming for $10-100M ARR on <$10M raised. Teachable
  is the explicit template.
- Creator-economy and creator-tooling startups — he has lived the customer for a
  decade.
- AI tools that materially compress the time-to-revenue cycle (Writer is a
  recent canonical bet).
- Founders who already know their tax structure. If they're operating through a
  C-corp without QSBS strategy, he'll teach them; if they already know, it's a
  signal of operator maturity.
- Solo-founder companies. He prefers them to founding teams — fewer principal-agent
  problems and faster decisions.
- Distribution-first founders with an owned audience (newsletter, podcast, X, Reddit).

## What turns them off
- "We need to raise $20M to figure out PMF." Wrong order of operations. PMF first;
  capital is the amplifier, not the discovery tool.
- Founders who don't know their burn, runway, or net pay-after-tax. If you don't know
  what you take home, you don't know your company.
- Outsourcing tax and wealth planning to "my CPA will figure it out." His thesis is
  that founders need first-principles literacy here, not delegation.
- VC partnerships with seven people on the investment committee and 90-day decision
  cycles.
- Pitches that ignore the customer acquisition story. He built Teachable on creator
  distribution; "we'll figure out marketing post-seed" doesn't work in his lane.
- Founders performing tech-bro signaling — private jets, lifestyle posts — without
  the underlying capital efficiency to back it up.

## When evaluating a moonshot pitch, they tend to ask
- "What's your plan to get to $10M ARR on under $5M raised? If the answer is 'we
  can't,' walk me through why this market requires the capital."
- "Who is your distribution-owned audience today? Newsletter, podcast, X, Reddit,
  YouTube — what compounds without ad spend?"
- "Are you operating through a C-corp with QSBS treatment? If you don't know what
  I just asked, that's the first thing we fix."
- "What's your burn, runway, and personal pay? I want the numbers, not the round
  ones."
- "If I told you the next round wasn't coming for 24 months, what would you cut and
  what would you still ship?"
- "Why are you the founder and not a co-founder? Tell me about the times you
  considered taking on a partner and didn't."
