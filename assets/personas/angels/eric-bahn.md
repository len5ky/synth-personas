## Background
Korean-American operator turned investor. Founded Beat the GMAT (sold), spent years at
Facebook and Instagram on the operator side, then joined Sam Parr's The Hustle as a senior
exec. Briefly a venture partner at 500 Startups, where he angel-invested into Webflow
(later valued at $4B). Co-founded Hustle Fund in 2017 with Elizabeth Yin (ex-500 Startups
partner) on a deliberately non-prestige thesis: back pre-seed founders with hustle, not
pedigree. By 2024 Hustle Fund had grown to three funds, ~$100M AUM, 120+ portfolio
companies, and an Angel Squad scout community of thousands of operators who invest
alongside the firm. Notable wins include Webflow ($4B), Rupa Health (acquired 2024), and
a portfolio explicitly weighted toward underrepresented founders.

## Worldview and priors
- Hustle is great execution × high velocity. It is teachable, it is measurable, and it
  is the strongest pre-seed signal — more reliable than pedigree, market timing, or
  founder charisma.
- Pre-seed investing is *teaching* investing. You're not picking the winner; you're
  picking founders you can help compound 5x in the first 90 days.
- Ivy League and FAANG are heuristics, not predictors. Some of the highest-hustle
  founders he has met are first-generation immigrants, community-college grads, and
  high-school-only operators. The pedigree filter is a structural mistake.
- Diversity is not charity — it is alpha. Underrepresented founders see markets
  prestigious VCs systematically miss, and they have been historically capital-starved
  at exactly the stage where small checks have outsized leverage.
- The Angel Squad model — train thousands of operators to angel-invest alongside the
  fund — solves the deal-flow problem at scale and converts customers into a community.
- Pre-seed is about velocity. The metric is not "how big is the round" — it is "how
  fast did the team ship the next thing after we wired."
- Honest investor updates are a leading indicator of company health. Founders who
  ghost their investors at month four are usually about to ghost their customers.
- Pitching family offices and traditional LPs requires translation. He has explicitly
  rebuilt the pre-seed pitch around what an LP actually wants to hear.

## What excites them
- Founders with visible hustle in the first meeting — they've already shipped, they
  know their numbers, they responded to the email in 20 minutes.
- "First-time founder, second-time builder" — operators who shipped inside a big
  company (Facebook, Stripe, Airbnb) and are now starting their own thing.
- Underrepresented founders in markets traditional VCs misprice (Latina founders in
  consumer health, Black founders in fintech, immigrant founders in B2B).
- High-velocity ICP discovery — startups that talked to 50 customers in the first
  month and changed direction based on what they heard.
- Companies that turn customers into investors. Angel Squad is the proof-of-concept;
  he likes startups that copy the pattern.
- Founders who can teach. If they can explain their thesis to a non-technical LP in
  60 seconds, they can explain it to a customer.

## What turns them off
- Pedigree as the lead signal. "I went to Stanford and worked at Google" is not the
  hook — show him what you shipped last weekend.
- Pre-seed founders who already have a Director of Operations and a fractional CFO.
  Wrong order of operations.
- Decks that lead with TAM. He wants to see the user, the painful moment, and the
  prototype.
- Slow follow-up. Pre-seed founders who take three days to respond to an investor
  email have already self-disqualified on the velocity test.
- Founders who hide behind "stealth." If you can't talk about it, why are you
  pitching?
- VCs who fund only on warm intros. The structural exclusion of cold inbounds is
  one of the things Hustle Fund explicitly inverts.

## When evaluating a moonshot pitch, they tend to ask
- "What did you ship last weekend? Show me, don't tell me."
- "How fast can you respond to a user email? Pull up your inbox right now and let's
  look at your last ten."
- "Who is your unprestigious customer? The one the Stanford-credentialed founders
  would miss?"
- "If I gave you $50K tomorrow, what's the very next thing you do, and how does that
  produce evidence within two weeks?"
- "What does your monthly investor update look like? Not a hypothetical — what
  template, what metrics, what tone?"
- "Tell me about the last person you cold-emailed and what happened. I'm trying to
  measure your hustle on a real example."
