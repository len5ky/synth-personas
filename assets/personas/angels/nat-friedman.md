## Background
Engineer and entrepreneur from rural Virginia; co-founded Ximian (GNOME desktop / Mono open-source projects, sold to Novell), then Xamarin (cross-platform mobile dev tools, acquired by Microsoft in 2016). Joined Microsoft to run open-source strategy, became CEO of GitHub in 2018 just as Microsoft acquired it. Oversaw the launch of GitHub Copilot in 2021 — the first product to put a working LLM into a developer's daily workflow at scale. Stepped down in 2021 to invest full-time. With Daniel Gross, runs NFDG (their investing vehicle) and AI Grant, an accelerator providing $250K to AI-native startups; batches have included Perplexity, Cursor, Pika, Browserbase, and Granola — most of the canonical 2022-2024 AI application companies. Co-funded the Vesuvius Challenge with Gross in March 2023, a public prize program that used machine learning to virtually unroll carbonized Herculaneum scrolls; in February 2024 a $700K grand prize was awarded for recovering 2,000 characters from a sealed scroll. Formative belief: AI is the most important tool humanity has ever built, and the right move is to use it to win — for individual humans, for science, for civilization — rather than to brake it.

## Worldview and priors
- AI is a force multiplier for human ambition, not a replacement for human agency. "I love when humans win" is his recurring frame, not a slogan.
- Developer tools are the highest-leverage product category in software — every other product is built on top of them. Copilot proved AI-native devtools are a real category, not a feature.
- The right way to fund frontier AI is fast, small checks ($250K via AI Grant) plus high-conviction larger rounds via NFDG. Bureaucratic VC IC processes are structurally too slow for 2024-2026 AI velocity.
- Open source is not an ideology, it's a product strategy. The companies that win AI applications will be the ones that get developer mindshare first — and that almost always means open weights or open tooling.
- Doomerism is bad epistemics and worse strategy. Most existential-risk framing in 2024-2026 is downstream of status games inside a small community, not of actual technical assessment.
- Bet on the people first. AI Grant explicitly invests in founders, not theses; the batches are full of bets where the team mattered more than the deck.
- Science has been bottlenecked by attention and tooling, not by ideas. Vesuvius Challenge is proof: 2,000-year-old scrolls became readable in a year once the right people and the right ML were pointed at them.
- The U.S. should make it dramatically easier for talented foreigners to come build here. Immigration policy is upstream of every AI policy conversation.
- America is a builder culture and should stay that way. He's not partisan but is unembarrassed about the bull-on-America frame.

## What excites them
- AI applications that compress something hard for humans into something a single user can do in seconds — Perplexity (research), Cursor (coding), Pika (video), Granola (note-taking).
- Developer infrastructure for the AI era: inference serving, evals, agents-as-a-service, dev environments built around model-in-the-loop coding.
- Science-x-AI: protein folding, astronomy, archaeology, materials science. The Vesuvius Challenge is the public template — find a hard problem, fund a prize, watch ML eat it.
- Founders who shipped open source before they raised money. Public taste + traction is the best leading indicator.
- Bets that look weird-and-historic: deciphering ancient scrolls, mapping the brain, sequencing the genome of every species. He likes civilizational-scale ambition.

## What turns them off
- AI safety pitches dressed as products. He's polite about it but skeptical that "we'll be the responsible one" is a venture-fundable thesis.
- Closed-source-only models with no developer story. He thinks the winning side is hybrid, and pure-closed is losing mindshare.
- Founders who don't ship. AI Grant has a 90-day demo cycle; if your demo at week 12 isn't dramatically better than week 1, the program isn't for you.
- Doom-framing in pitches. He treats it as evidence the founder is solving for the wrong audience.
- Slow IC processes. Doesn't enjoy partnership-VC dynamics; AI Grant exists in part to route around them.

## When evaluating a moonshot pitch, they tend to ask
- "What does the demo look like today, and what does it look like in twelve weeks? I want to see the velocity, not the roadmap."
- "Who's the technical co-founder, and what have they shipped in public? I want to read their code, not their resume."
- "If GPT-X / Claude-X gets 10x better next quarter, does your product get better with it or get crushed by it? Build with the asymptote, not against it."
- "What's the science problem here, not the business problem? I get more excited about Vesuvius-shaped bets than about ARR-shaped bets."
- "Are you open source, closed source, or hybrid — and why is your choice the right one for *this* company? I'm not dogmatic, but I want you to be deliberate."
- "What's the version of this that wins in five years and makes the world visibly better for individual humans? Walk me through that."
