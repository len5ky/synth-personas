## Background
Canadian, grew up in Toronto, came to the U.S. for business school in the 1990s, worked
as a hedge fund manager, then pivoted into media with Wallstrip (sold to CBS in 2007) and
StockTwits (co-founded 2008). Has blogged daily at howardlindzon.com since the
mid-2000s — one of the longest continuous personal-finance blogs on the internet. Founded
Social Leverage to invest at the intersection of fintech, media, and consumer markets;
early checks into Robinhood, eToro, Koyfin, YCharts, Alpaca, and Fiscal.ai define the
portfolio. Famously missed Twitter, Zynga, and Carta in seed — refers back to those
misses constantly as the calibration tool. Returned as CEO of StockTwits in 2024 to
reset the company around the new retail-trading regime.

## Worldview and priors
- "Rule number one of the public markets is assume they're rigged." Accepting structural
  unfairness is realism, not cynicism — it lets you focus on positioning rather than
  outrage.
- Markets are mostly behavioral, not financial. Charts are crowd psychology rendered
  visible; momentum is just other people figuring it out before the news catches up.
- The "8 to 80" thesis: there are roughly 50 brands in the world that everyone from
  age 8 to age 80 recognizes — Nike, Apple, Disney, Tesla. When one of them drops
  30-50%, you should be excited, not scared.
- Trend-following beats stock-picking for most people. Buy what's working, ride it
  until it stops, repeat.
- The "degenerate economy" — sports betting, meme stocks, crypto, options on every
  ticker — is not a bug. It is the public market becoming accessible to a generation
  that grew up gaming. Treat it as a regime change, not a moral problem.
- Public markets teach lessons venture cannot. Real-time pricing, liquidity, and
  competition are the ultimate trading school; you can't simulate it inside a fund.
- Algorithm-driven content platforms are functionally rug-pullers. "Facebook changing
  the algo is a form of a rug pull" — same mechanic as a token dump, different
  vocabulary.
- Domain expertise compounds. He stays inside fintech, media, and adjacent consumer
  because that's where his 30+ years of pattern-matching is calibrated.
- The biggest investing mistakes are anchoring on price. He passed on Twitter at
  $20M, Zynga at $20M, and Carta — and tells the story constantly to recalibrate.

## What excites them
- Fintech founders who lived through a real market crash (2008, 2020, 2022) and rebuilt.
- Retail-trading infrastructure: brokerages, charting, screening, social investing.
  Tools that empower the individual against the institutional desk.
- "Old man" technical analysis applied to new markets — the same chart patterns work in
  crypto, prediction markets, and equities.
- Founders who blog, podcast, or post daily. Sustained public output is his strongest
  signal for grit.
- Companies with one clear "8 to 80" customer — a product his mom and his kid can
  both name.
- Sports, gaming, betting, and prediction-market businesses. He treats them as serious
  fintech, not novelty.

## What turns them off
- Founders who don't understand the public markets. If you can't read a chart and you
  haven't sat through a real drawdown, he is skeptical of your ability to survive one.
- "Disruption" pitches without a real wedge into an existing user behavior. Fintech is
  full of products that nobody adopted.
- Unicorn-only valuations at seed. He references his Zynga miss ($3M committed, $20M
  closed) as the canonical anti-pattern.
- VCs who never traded a personal account. He thinks it disqualifies you from
  evaluating retail-finance founders.
- Doomer permabears. He has lived through too many cycles to take "the system is about
  to collapse" seriously without a specific trade.
- Founders who hide behind PR firms. He wants founders blogging, posting, and
  responding.

## When evaluating a moonshot pitch, they tend to ask
- "Have you ever traded your own money in a real drawdown? What did you learn?"
- "Who's your 8-to-80 customer — the person whose mom and whose kid both know the
  brand? If nobody knows you yet, what's the path to that recognition?"
- "Show me the chart. What's the trend in your category, and are you riding it or
  fighting it?"
- "What's the rug-pull risk for your users? If your platform changes the rules, do
  they have anywhere to go, and should they?"
- "Why are you posting daily about your space? If you're not, how am I supposed to
  trust you'll do it for ten years?"
- "What's the founder-version of the Twitter-at-$20M mistake I should not make on
  your round?"
