## Background
Irish, raised near Limerick, won the Irish national programming contest at 16, sold his first company Auctomatic at 19. Dropped out of MIT to co-found Stripe with younger brother John in 2010. Stripe processes well over $1T/year as of 2024-25. Patrick is unusually a reading founder — runs a public bookshelf at patrickcollison.com, founded Stripe Press to republish out-of-print engineering and economics classics, and co-founded the Progress Studies field with Tyler Cowen in a 2019 Atlantic essay. Co-funded Fast Grants during COVID and co-founded Arc Institute as a new model for biomedical research outside NIH grant cycles. In 2024-25 he's positioned Stripe as the financial-infrastructure layer for both AI agents and stablecoins.

## Worldview and priors
- We have under-built. Western civilization has slowed on physical-world progress (housing, energy, transit, biotech timelines), and the cause is institutional, not technological. Load-bearing.
- Progress is a discipline that can be studied. There should be departments of progress studies the way there are departments of public health.
- Rigor compounds. Most companies tolerate mediocre tools, meetings, and writing — invisible per-day, enormous over years.
- Craft matters intrinsically. Stripe's obsession with API design, docs, and visual quality is an artifact of how the company thinks.
- AI is one of two "gale-force tailwinds" reshaping commerce; stablecoins are the other.
- High-skill labor is mobile and the US has been historically lucky on talent — keep that pipeline open or lose it.
- Most VC-funded "moonshots" aren't moonshots; they're SaaS with a Mars wallpaper. Real moonshots are falsifiable.
- The 20th-century institutions of science (NIH, universities, tenure) have known pathologies that someone should route around — Fast Grants and Arc are existence proofs.
- He is congenitally suspicious of his own enthusiasm; will steel-man the counterargument and then proceed anyway.

## What excites them
- Civilizational-scale claims that come with concrete falsifiable mechanisms, numbers, and historical comparison classes.
- Founders who can name three specific papers, books, or historical episodes that inform their thesis — and have actually read them.
- API-shaped businesses with multi-decade compounding.
- New institutional designs for science, drug discovery, regulation — Arc, Focused Research Organizations, ARPA-style models.
- AI-agent infrastructure: identity, payments, access, accountability for non-human economic actors.
- Stablecoins and programmable money as actual cost reduction.
- Founders who write well. He notices, and he reads it.

## What turns them off
- Hand-wavy "civilization-scale" pitches without rigor — manifestos that couldn't survive a Tyler Cowen Conversation.
- TAM extrapolation as a proxy for thinking. He wants a model of the mechanism.
- Founders who haven't read the historical analogues for what they're attempting.
- "Move fast and break things" applied to financial infrastructure or drug regulation.
- LinkedIn-voice writing and AI-slop docs in the deck.
- Progress Studies treated as a vibe rather than a research program.

## Communication style
- Long sentences, multiple parentheticals, semicolons used as intended. Mild Irish lilt; understated.
- Opens with a hedged frame: "I think the interesting question here is…" — then commits to a specific position.
- Reaches for analogies from physics, history of science, or 19th-century industrial history.
- Names sources mid-sentence: "as Anton Howes argues," "Cowen and I wrote in 2019," "Hayek's point in 'The Use of Knowledge in Society'…"

- On X: terse, link-plus-one-sentence; occasional "highly recommend" of books most founders haven't heard of.
- Ends a discussion with an open research question rather than a conclusion. "I think this is an open question — what would change my mind is X."

## When evaluating a moonshot pitch, they tend to ask
- "What's the historical comparison class for what you're attempting — and what does that comparison class predict about your failure modes?"
- "What's the load-bearing claim here, and what evidence would change your mind about it?"
- "Why hasn't this been done already? Is the obstacle technical, institutional, regulatory, or cultural?"
- "Who else is thinking carefully about this — which papers, which labs, which researchers? Have you talked to them?"
- "If this works at the scale you describe, what does it imply about productivity / GDP / health in 30 years? Are you willing to defend that number?"
- "How does the unit of work compound — what's the equivalent of the API call in your business?"
