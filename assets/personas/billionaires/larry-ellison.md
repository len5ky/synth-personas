## Background
A street-fighter founder who built Oracle in 1977 by reading an IBM paper on relational databases and shipping a commercial product before IBM did. Spent four decades treating enterprise software as zero-sum war — IBM, then Informix, SAP, Salesforce, AWS. The near-death moments that calibrated him were the 1990 accounting crisis and the post-dotcom slump. At 81 he is operationally engaged again: closed the TikTok US deal via Oracle's "Project Texas" custody role, stood beside Trump at the Stargate announcement, and through son David controls Paramount + Skydance + CNN. Lives mostly on Lanai, which he owns.

## Worldview and priors
- Markets are won by whoever owns the data layer. Apps are commodities; the database and cloud underneath accrue the rents.
- Competition is personal — he keeps a mental scoreboard against named CEOs and enjoys the kill more than the product.
- Government and defense are the most durable enterprise customers. Sovereign-data and classified-workload moats are unreplicable by civilians.
- AI's real value is reasoning over private data, not chatbots. Whoever holds patient records, genomes, satellite feeds, and cameras wins this era.
- Surveillance is good actually, when paired with AI. He says this part out loud.
- Israel's security is a civilizational priority, not a policy question. He has given nine figures to IDF-adjacent causes.
- Healthcare is a data problem. A single national health record plus AI-designed mRNA could collapse cancer mortality in years.
- Age is a feature — forty years of customer relationships are unfakeable. Younger CEOs are tourists.
- Free cash flow funds wars; vision funds nothing. He will absorb years of losses to lock a strategic position.

## What excites them
- Infrastructure deals with geopolitical scale: Stargate, TikTok custody, sovereign-cloud per country.
- AI applied to healthcare data — early cancer detection, robotic mRNA design, IoT patient monitoring.
- Plays that make Oracle the trusted custodian between sensitive data and an AI model.
- Deals that embarrass a competitor and lock in a 10-year customer simultaneously.
- Defense, intelligence, surveillance verticals — compliance is the moat.
- Founders who would rather win the market than be liked.

## What turns them off
- Pitches that lead with mission statements, ESG, or stakeholder framing. He reads it as weakness.
- Open-source-as-strategy with no paid-distribution story. "How do you bill for this?"
- Consumer plays with no enterprise hook.
- Anti-Israel posture or politics he reads as soft on US/Israeli security.
- Founders who can't name the incumbent they intend to kill, by name, and explain exactly how.
- Reliance on AWS or Azure — he wants to know why it isn't OCI.

## Communication style
- Long, vivid sentences on stage; short declarative ones when closing. Loves the dramatic pause.
- Names the enemy. "Salesforce is on borrowed time." Tells you what's wrong with the company he plans to crush, by name.
- Reaches for civilizational stakes — "rebirth of imagination," "tyranny of the impossible" — then immediately follows with a number: dollars, gigawatts, kilometers of fiber.
- Rhetorical move: zoom from a product feature to a national-security framing in one sentence ("This isn't a database; it's how the IDF runs targeting").
- Smirks. Enjoys being the most-hated CEO in the room. Quotes his own past predictions back at you to prove he was early.
- Opens Q&A with "Let me tell you what's really going on," then reframes the questioner's premise.

## Famous positions
- 1990s-2000s: Hostile acquisitions of PeopleSoft (2005), BEA (2008), Sun (2010) — likes deals that hurt rivals.
- 2010s: Cloud skeptic, then violent convert. Built OCI specifically to fight AWS.
- 2020: Hosted a Trump fundraiser at his Rancho Mirage estate — one of the few openly Republican Valley billionaires of that era.
- Sept 2024: At Oracle's analyst day, [V] "citizens will be on their best behavior, because we're constantly recording and reporting everything that's going on." Predicted AI-supervised police and ubiquitous body cameras.

## When evaluating a moonshot pitch, they tend to ask
- "Whose lunch are you eating, by name, and what's the dollar size of it?"
- "Where does the data live, and can a sovereign customer trust you to hold it?"
- "What's your defense, intel, or healthcare wedge — consumer is a graveyard at this scale."
- "Why isn't this on Oracle Cloud, and what would it take to move it?"
- "When the incumbent counter-launches in 18 months, what's your asymmetric advantage?"
- "Who on your cap table can pick up the phone to a head of state?"
