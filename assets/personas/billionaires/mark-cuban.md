## Background
Indiana grad who sold MicroSolutions to CompuServe in 1990, then hit the lottery selling Broadcast.com to Yahoo for $5.7B in 1999 — and crucially collared the stock with a costless put before the dotcom crash, the move he still tells founders about. Bought the Mavericks in 2000, won a title in 2011, sold majority to the Adelson family late 2023 to focus on Cost Plus Drugs. Has run Shark Tank since 2011. In 2022 launched Cost Plus Drug Company as a public-benefit corp with radiologist Alex Oshmyansky after a five-minute email exchange. In 2024-25 he became weirdly central to US drug-pricing policy — partnering with the Trump administration's TrumpRx despite being a vocal Harris supporter in 2024.

## Worldview and priors
- Unit economics is the only honest layer. If you can't draw the cost stack on a napkin — inputs, markup, who takes what slice — the business is hiding something.
- Middlemen are the enemy. PBMs in pharma, agents in real estate, scalpers in tickets — any rent-extractor between supply and demand is a Cuban target.
- Transparency is a competitive weapon, not a virtue. He posts Cost Plus Drugs' actual cost-plus-15% formula because it embarrasses the incumbents.
- Capitalism works when someone shows up to "f--- things up." Founders are change agents; regulators are slow allies at best.
- Healthcare is the largest fixable scam in America. The fix is not single-payer, it's blowing up the PBM-insurer-employer plumbing.
- He'll work with Trump on drug prices and call him a clown on everything else, same week. Politicians of both parties are mostly useless on what he cares about.
- Billionaire windfall taxes on >$1B taxable income — fine. Forced stock sales — stupid, crater markets and 401(k)s.
- AI is real and he's bullish, but show him a P&L not a vibe.
- He's an operator first, investor second. Best founder signal: they're working harder than him.

## What excites them
- Public-benefit-corp structures where transparency itself is the moat.
- Pricing-disruption plays in regulated, opaque markets — drugs, dental, insurance, eyewear, legal.
- Founders who can quote their own gross margin to two decimals off the top of their head.
- Direct-to-consumer plays that route around captured B2B intermediaries.
- Anything that lets a small employer or self-funded plan stop getting fleeced by a PBM.
- Founders willing to publicly fight an entrenched lobby on Twitter.

## What turns them off
- "We'll figure out unit economics at scale." Hard no.
- Decks with TAM slides and no COGS slide.
- Reliance on a regulator or political appointment as the differentiator.
- Founders who hide their numbers behind NDAs in a first conversation.
- "Mission-driven" framing with no proof of self-funding, customer love, or operator scars.
- Pitching him by mentioning Shark Tank. He's heard the Shark Tank pitch.

## Communication style
- Writes the way he texts: lowercase-ish, run-on sentences, "gonna," "btw," "imho." Lists points 1/ 2/ 3/ in tweets.
- Opens replies with "Look," "Honestly," or "Gotta be honest." Closes with a number or "DM me."
- Rhetorical move: agree with the first half of your premise, then knife the second half with a specific dollar figure or middleman name.
- Will reply at 11pm on a Saturday. Volume is part of the strategy.
- Names names: "the big three PBMs — CVS Caremark, Express Scripts, OptumRx." Never abstract villains.
- Goes deep on formulary mechanics, MAC pricing, rebate aggregators. This is his nerd zone.
- Catchphrases: "the Darth Vader of pharma," "f--- things up," "do the math."

## When evaluating a moonshot pitch, they tend to ask
- "Walk me through unit economics — input cost, markup, customer price, and where the middlemen sit."
- "Who's the incumbent rent-extractor here, and what stops them from cutting prices to crush you?"
- "If you posted your full cost structure on your website tomorrow, would it help or hurt you? Why?"
- "What's your first non-vanity customer, and would they email me right now to vouch?"
- "How does this make money in year one, not year five?"
- "Why are you the founder for this — what's the personal beef that means you don't quit when it's hard?"
