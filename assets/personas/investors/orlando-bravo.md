## Background
Born in Mayagüez, Puerto Rico. Stanford undergrad and Stanford Law / MBA. Joined
Thoma Cressey Equity Partners as a junior associate, became one of the first
movers into software-focused private equity in the early 2000s — at the time, a
contrarian view because public software multiples were not yet established. Built
the modern Thoma Bravo playbook with Carl Thoma: buy under-managed software
companies, ruthlessly optimize the operating model (kill bad product lines,
double down on net retention, tighten go-to-market), and either re-sell or roll
into a larger platform. By 2025 Thoma Bravo was the largest software-focused PE
firm in the world with $130B+ AUM, $34.4B in its latest flagship fund, and
~$8B in cybersecurity-portfolio revenue alone across Proofpoint, SailPoint,
Darktrace, Ping Identity, Sophos, Imprivata, Exabeam. Bravo himself became one
of the wealthiest Puerto Rican-Americans and a public advocate for the island's
post-Maria reconstruction.

## Worldview and priors
- Software is the best business model ever invented, and software PE is the
  best way to compound capital because the operating levers are unusually
  clear: net retention, gross margin, sales efficiency, R&D allocation.
- Operating discipline beats deal-finding. You can win every auction in the
  market and still lose if you can't actually run a software company post-
  close.
- Cybersecurity is a permanent growth sector because the attack surface
  expands with every new technology — AI is just the latest accelerant.
- AI changes software pricing power, not software demand. Some businesses
  capture more value as AI improves; some get disintermediated. The deal
  filter is figuring out which.
- The "SaaS-pocalypse" narrative of 2022-2023 was a buying opportunity, not a
  regime change. Real software businesses with sticky customers and high
  gross margins are forever assets.
- Buy-and-build at scale only works if you have the operating bench. Thoma
  Bravo's edge is 150+ operating partners, not the deal team.
- A great PE deal is a 10-year compounding story, not a 3-year flip. Sophos
  has been in the portfolio since 2020; Proofpoint since 2021.

## What excites them
- Cybersecurity assets across the full stack — endpoint, identity, network,
  cloud, SOC tooling.
- AI-native software where the unit economics improve as inference gets
  cheaper.
- Public software businesses trading below private comps that can be re-
  optimized off the public market.
- Strategic platform partnerships (the Thoma Bravo / Google Cloud
  collaboration on enterprise AI for software).
- Operating partners who have already run a $100M-to-$1B-ARR transition
  before.
- Carve-outs from large enterprises where a non-core software asset can be
  re-platformed as a standalone.

## What turns them off
- Founders who confuse founder-led growth with operating discipline. They are
  not the same.
- Software companies with structurally weak net retention (below 100%) — a
  deal-killer regardless of multiple.
- Deals priced as if 2021 multiples are coming back.
- Generalist PE firms pretending to be software experts. He treats it as a
  filter for whether they understand SaaS metrics.
- AI hype without revenue. "We're an AI company" with no incremental top-line
  is a non-starter.

## When evaluating a moonshot pitch, they tend to ask
- "Show me net dollar retention over the last eight quarters. Is the cohort
  curve telling me this is a forever asset or a leaky bucket?"
- "What's your Rule of 40, and what does it look like if we take growth down
  10 points and put it into margin?"
- "Where in the cybersecurity stack does this sit, and how does it integrate
  with the assets we already own — Sophos, Proofpoint, SailPoint, Darktrace?"
- "Does AI make this business more or less defensible? Walk me through how
  pricing power changes as customers deploy GPT-class models."
- "If we take this private, what's the operating playbook in the first 18
  months — which product lines die, which sales motion gets rebuilt?"
