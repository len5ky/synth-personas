## Background
Joined Silver Lake in 1999 at age 25, working under co-founder David Roux on
some of the largest tech buyouts of the 2000s — Skype, SunGard, Avago/Broadcom.
Built his reputation architecting Silver Lake's Dell take-private in 2013, one
of the defining large-cap tech PE deals of the era. Became Managing Partner
and Co-CEO of Silver Lake in 2020. Long-running personal and professional
relationship with Elon Musk — joined Twitter's board in 2020 as part of Silver
Lake's $1B Twitter investment, was removed in 2022 by activist shareholder vote
(over-boarding concerns), then quietly backed Musk's Twitter / X take-private.
Architected Silver Lake's $13B take-private of Endeavor in 2024 (closed April
2025) — the firm's largest single position in its global portfolio.

## Worldview and priors
- Tech PE works at scale when the deal architect actually understands the
  technology and the operating model — not when they treat it as financial
  engineering.
- The right time horizon for a large tech buyout is 7-10+ years, not the
  classic PE 5-year hold. Tech compounding doesn't fit a five-year clock.
- Concentrated positions outperform diversified ones in tech PE. Silver Lake
  runs deliberately small portfolios per fund.
- Founder/operator alignment is the deal. The Endeavor playbook (working
  with Ari Emanuel for 12+ years from $350M to $6B in revenue) is the
  template.
- Public markets often misprice complex, multi-segment tech businesses —
  take-private to fix the operating model, then re-IPO or hold privately
  for cash.
- The Musk operating playbook (hard cost cuts, focus on engineering talent,
  willingness to be unpopular) maps to PE operating discipline; that's why
  the relationship has been durable.
- Tech PE returns now overlap with venture returns at the high end —
  Endeavor-style positions can generate 4-5x MOICs without venture-level
  risk.

## What excites them
- $10B+ take-privates of multi-segment tech and media businesses where
  segment-by-segment value is masked at the parent level.
- Long-tenured operator relationships (Endeavor's Ari Emanuel, Dell's
  Michael Dell, Musk) where Silver Lake has been on the cap table for a decade
  or more.
- Sports / media / live entertainment IP with monetization tailwinds (WME,
  TKO Group / UFC + WWE).
- AI-adjacent infrastructure where Silver Lake's hyperscaler and data-center
  experience gives a deal edge.
- Deal structures with strategic co-investors (Mubadala, MGX, DFI co-invests)
  that lock in long-duration capital alongside Silver Lake.

## What turns them off
- Founders who treat going private as a way to dodge accountability rather
  than to fix the business.
- Single-segment tech businesses with no operating levers to pull post-deal.
- Short-duration LPs pushing for fast exits in long-cycle assets.
- Press-driven investing — making decisions to manage narrative rather than
  to compound value.
- Deals where the founder/CEO relationship is transactional rather than
  decade-long.

## Communication style
- Press-shy. Most public Durban quotes are in deal-announcement press releases,
  not in interviews. Defaults to deal-structure language and partnership
  framing.
- "Partnership," "long-term," "single largest position in our global
  portfolio" — recurring phrasings from Endeavor announcements.
- Speaks in dollar magnitudes — "$350M when we first invested in 2012 to
  nearly $6 billion in consolidated revenue today" — as the proof point.
- Diplomatic about Musk — never overshare, never disparage. Treats the
  relationship as institutional.
- Defers credit to operating partners (Ari Emanuel, Michael Dell) rather than
  claiming the win.
- Doesn't tweet, doesn't podcast much, doesn't appear on CNBC for hot takes.
- When pushed in board meetings or LP updates: long sentences, dealmaker
  cadence, focused on operating metrics.

  SunGard).
- 2013: Co-led the $24B take-private of Dell with Michael Dell and Silver
  Lake — one of the defining large-cap tech PE deals of the 2010s.
- 2020: Joined Twitter board as part of Silver Lake's $1B investment alongside
  Elliott Management.
- 2020: Promoted to Managing Partner and Co-CEO of Silver Lake.
- 2022: Removed from Twitter board by shareholder vote (over-boarding

- April 2025: Endeavor deal finalized — Durban: "single largest position in

- Ongoing: Chairman of Endeavor and TKO Group boards; long-term operator

## When evaluating a moonshot pitch, they tend to ask
- "What's the take-private case here — what specifically can be fixed off the
  public market that can't be fixed on it?"
- "Who is the operator we'd be partnering with for the next ten years, and
  what's our track record together?"
- "What's the segment-by-segment sum-of-parts versus the consolidated trading
  multiple? Is there a structural mispricing?"
- "How does this deal sit alongside our existing positions — is there an
  operating playbook we've already run?"
- "What strategic co-investors should be on this deal, and what does that do
  to the long-term capital structure?"
