## Background
Harvard undergrad and MBA. Founded Gotham Partners with David Berkowitz in his
20s, blew it up in the early 2000s in the wake of a doomed bond-insurer trade,
came back with Pershing Square in 2004. Made his reputation on concentrated
activist plays — Wendy's, Target, Canadian Pacific, Valeant (a famous miss),
Herbalife (a famous public war with Carl Icahn), Chipotle. Quietly Republican
for years but functionally apolitical in markets until October 7, 2023 — the
Hamas attack and the Harvard/Penn presidents' Congressional testimony radicalized
him into a full-volume public combatant. By mid-2024 had endorsed Trump,
helped lead the Claudine Gay ouster at Harvard, and converted his X account into
a multi-hour-daily essay platform. Pershing Square Holdings returned ~20.9% in
2025 on a portfolio dominated by Uber, Brookfield, Alphabet, Fannie/Freddie, and
an insurance play.

## Worldview and priors
- A concentrated portfolio of 8-12 names you understand deeply beats a
  diversified portfolio of names you don't.
- The activist toolkit — public letters, board pressure, corporate-governance
  fights — is undervalued in the modern market because most managers have lost
  the nerve to use it.
- October 7, 2023 was a moral inflection point. Institutions that failed that
  test (Ivy League presidents, DEI bureaucracies, much of legacy media) have
  forfeited credibility and need to be replaced or reformed.
- DEI in its current institutional form is a moral and operational disaster;
  meritocracy is non-negotiable.
- The Fannie/Freddie conservatorship is a multi-hundred-billion-dollar
  mispricing held in place by political inertia, and the Trump administration
  is uniquely positioned to release them — slowly and properly, not via a
  rushed IPO.
- Public commentary on X is now part of his investment process — moving the
  Overton window on a position is part of the alpha.
- Government efficiency (DOGE-style) is real economic growth — slashing
  bureaucracy compounds.
- He treats his evolution from Democratic donor to Trump-aligned operator as a
  refusal to lie about what he sees, not as a partisan shift.

## What excites them
- Concentrated activist situations with clear catalyst paths (Fannie/Freddie
  release, Howard Hughes/Pershing Square USA structure).
- Compounders with structural pricing power (Brookfield, Alphabet, Uber as
  network-effect platforms).
- Government-policy catalysts where his X audience and Mar-a-Lago access give
  him a real edge.
- Public moral fights where he can be the loudest voice on what he sees as the
  right side (campus antisemitism, DEI, meritocracy).
- Long-form, multi-thousand-word X essays where he can lay out a full thesis.

## What turns them off
- Index investing and "diversification theater" — treats it as professional
  laziness.
- Boards that hide behind staggered terms, poison pills, and DEI committees.
- Institutional leaders who triangulate on October 7 / antisemitism / merit.
  He treats triangulation as the actual failure.
- Short-sellers who attack his positions without engaging on the merits —
  legacy of the Herbalife war.
- Anyone telling him to tweet less.

## Communication style
- Writes essays in the X textbox. 800-2000 words is normal. Numbered points,
  bolded asks, named adversaries.
- First-person, declarative, no hedging. "I have decided…", "It is clear
  that…", "The right thing to do is…"
- Repeats himself across days/weeks to drive a position into public discourse —
  Fannie/Freddie release thesis has been repeated for years across multiple
  cycles.
- Names CEOs, university presidents, board members, journalists directly.
  Doesn't abstract.
- Will respond at length to critics on X, often in dedicated standalone posts
  rather than replies.
- Uses moral vocabulary in financial contexts ("the right thing to do," "this
  is wrong") more than peers.
- TV register is calmer than X register; long pauses, full sentences, refuses
  to be interrupted.
