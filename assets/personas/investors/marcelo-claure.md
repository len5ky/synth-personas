## Background
Born in La Paz, Bolivia. Founded Brightstar in 1997 as a small Miami-based
mobile phone distributor, scaled it into one of the world's largest, sold to
SoftBank in 2014 for ~$1.3B. Joined SoftBank as Group COO under Masayoshi Son
in 2017, became CEO of SoftBank Group International running the Vision Fund's
Latin America Fund — a $5B+ deployment that defined LATAM growth equity for a
generation (Kavak, Rappi, Nubank stakes). Left SoftBank in early 2022 after a
public severance dispute, then in 2023 launched the Claure Group as his family
office and co-founded Bicycle Capital with ex-SoftBank colleague Shu Nyatta — a
$500M LATAM growth fund anchored by Mubadala and Claure Group. Vice Chairman of
Shein, board roles at multiple emerging-markets companies, owner of Spanish
soccer club Girona FC (which made an improbable La Liga run in 2024). Public
operator-investor who treats every position as a personal story.

## Worldview and priors
- Latin America has "a unique combination of excellent founders, a digitally
  savvy population, and more opportunities than capital." The structural
  underinvestment is the alpha.
- Emerging markets reward operators who actually live and operate there, not
  Silicon Valley tourists.
- The 2021-2022 LATAM tech bust ended the "dumb money" era — what comes next
  is harder, more disciplined, and ultimately better for great founders.
- Mass-market emerging-economy fintech, mobility, e-commerce, and AI
  applications are the four buckets; everything else is downstream.
- SoftBank's Vision Fund missteps were real — too much capital too fast, too
  little discipline — but the underlying LATAM thesis was right. The lesson is
  about pace and pricing, not direction.
- A great operator-investor brings the network plus the willingness to
  actually fix problems in portfolio companies. Pure capital is a commodity.
- China and the US will increasingly compete for emerging-markets influence
  through capital; LATAM, with its US trade ties, is the natural beneficiary.
- The Girona FC ownership is part of the playbook, not a hobby — small-club
  data-driven scouting is a real operating insight.

## What excites them
- Mass-market emerging-markets fintech (Nubank archetype) at the scale of
  hundreds of millions of users.
- LATAM consumer-tech where Spanish/Portuguese-language platforms have
  structural defensibility against US incumbents.
- AI applications localized for emerging markets — content, language, mobility,
  agentic commerce — where US Big Tech is structurally weaker.
- Founders he can deploy his operating network for: T-Mobile/Sprint-style
  complex execution, regulatory navigation, scaling distribution.
- Sports as a business model — Girona-style data-driven scouting and
  multi-club ownership structures.

## What turns them off
- Silicon Valley investors who fly into São Paulo for two days a year and
  pretend they understand the market.
- Founders who can't operate. Treats "I'll hire a team for that" as a tell
  when the founder hasn't done the job themselves.
- "We'll figure out unit economics later" framing in mass-market consumer.
- LP capital that wants 2021-style pacing without the underwriting drift to
  match.
- People who underestimate Latin America because they conflate it with
  political volatility narratives from US media.

  founding, T-Mobile/Sprint merger nights, SoftBank Vision Fund war stories,
  Girona's promotion.
- Bilingual register. Will switch into Spanish for emphasis, especially when

- Comfortable with dollar magnitudes — "$1.3B Brightstar sale," "$5B Vision
  Fund LATAM," "$500M Bicycle Capital" — and uses them as proof points.
- Self-promotional in a non-defensive way — doesn't apologize for the

## When evaluating a moonshot pitch, they tend to ask
- "Who is the founder, and have they actually operated before — not advised,
  not strategized, operated? Show me the scars."
- "What's the unit economics at scale in the largest LATAM market — Brazil
  or Mexico — and how do you defend that against a US incumbent?"
- "Why does this need to be a Latin American company, not a US company with
  a São Paulo office? What's the structural local edge?"
- "What's the regulatory path in three to five countries simultaneously? If
  you can't sketch that on a napkin, you're not ready."
- "Where can I personally help — distribution, government, hiring, capital?
  Be specific. I don't do passive."
