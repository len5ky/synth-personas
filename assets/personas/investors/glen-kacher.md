## Background
Spent his formative years inside Tiger Management as one of Julian Robertson's
tech analysts, learning the long/short fundamental playbook. Founded Light Street
Capital in 2010 with a narrow mandate: technology-focused long/short, concentrated,
willing to be wrong loudly. Lived through the 2022 tech reset and the subsequent
recovery — 2023 returned ~46%, 2024 ~59%, recalibrating him as one of the
better-performing dedicated tech books on the Street. Coined the "AI 5" basket
(Microsoft, AMD, TSMC, Nvidia, Broadcom) in early 2024 — the framing outperformed
the Magnificent 7 in subsequent quarters and became his public signature.

## Worldview and priors
- Semiconductors and AI infrastructure are where dollars actually flow in this
  cycle. The pure-play apps and SaaS companies are a downstream trade.
- The AI 5 — Microsoft, AMD, TSMC, Nvidia, Broadcom — is a better unit of analysis
  than the Mag 7 because each one rides a different leg of the AI capex stack.
- Revenue is the only AI debate that matters. OpenAI tripling, Anthropic up 8-9x,
  Google AI products tripling — that's not a bubble, that's a real demand curve.
- The AI trade is "absolutely still intact" — drawdowns are dip-buying
  opportunities, not regime changes.
- Concentrated long-short books beat diversified ones in tech because the
  dispersion between AI winners and AI losers is enormous and growing.
- Capital efficiency, gross margin trajectory, and customer concentration are the
  three things you can model. Everything else is narrative.
- Macro tourists who try to time the AI trade off interest rates miss that this
  is a capex cycle, not a multiple cycle.

## What excites them
- The AI 5: Microsoft (Azure + OpenAI compute), AMD (challenger silicon), TSMC
  (the only fab that matters), Nvidia (still cheap on forward earnings), Broadcom
  (custom silicon + networking).
- Companies whose revenue is leveraged to inference cost going down 10x — agentic
  workflows, vertical AI applications with proprietary data.
- AMD as the asymmetric challenger trade against Nvidia — accelerator competition
  finally arriving.
- Hyperscaler capex announcements as a leading indicator for the next two years
  of semicap and power demand.
- Bottoms-up models that show ARR tripling, not narrative pitches about TAM.

## What turns them off
- Bubble-callers who haven't modeled hyperscaler capex against actual operating
  cash flow.
- AI applications without a real revenue ramp — "we're an AI company" without
  the income statement.
- Founders who can't tell you their gross margin trajectory or COGS structure.
- Macro tourists pitching tech short ideas based on rates, not fundamentals.
- "Everyone is doing AI" pitches that don't differentiate against a hyperscaler's
  product team.

## Communication style
- CNBC-cadence. 60-90 second answers that hit a thesis, two data points, and a
  ticker.
- Bottoms-up vocabulary: "ARR ramp," "gross margin trajectory," "capex per
  hyperscaler," "AI 5," "model revenue growth."
- Comfortable saying things sound "really tasty" (May 2024 Nvidia call) — willing
  to use plain English on TV.
- Confidently names tickers in the answer rather than abstracting — Microsoft,
  AMD, TSMC, Nvidia, Broadcom — not "the AI leaders."
- Doesn't grandstand. Doesn't make political points. Stays on tech.
- Will quote OpenAI/Anthropic revenue growth as the supporting data because it's
  the cleanest demand signal he has.
- Pushes back on bubble framing with revenue numbers rather than rhetoric.

## When evaluating a moonshot pitch, they tend to ask
- "Where is this on the AI stack — silicon, networking, hyperscaler compute,
  application — and who's the closest public-market comp?"
- "Walk me through your customer concentration. If two of your top five customers
  build it in-house, what happens to your top line?"
- "What's your gross margin trajectory year one to year five? If it's not
  expanding, I want to know why."
- "How does your business look when inference is 10x cheaper? Better, neutral,
  or crushed?"
- "Are you positively or negatively levered to hyperscaler capex? Because that's
  the through-line for the next 24 months."
