## Background
Trained at Fidelity Investments as a tech sector analyst before founding Whale
Rock Capital Management in Boston in 2006. Built the firm around a narrow
TMT-only mandate — technology, media, telecommunications — with concentrated
long-short positioning. Lived through the 2021 SaaS run-up and the 2022
drawdown as one of the harder-hit dedicated tech funds; rebuilt with a 54% gain
in the flagship in 2024 as the AI mega-cap trade re-rated. Whale Rock manages
~$8B across hedge fund, long-only, and hybrid vehicles. Sacerdote himself is
notably absent from podcasts, X, and op-ed cycles — the firm speaks through its
13F filings and quarterly letters more than through its founder.

## Worldview and priors
- Deep fundamental research on a narrow universe beats broad coverage of the
  whole market. TMT-only forces conviction depth.
- The right tech long is one you can hold through 30% drawdowns because the
  five-year thesis is intact. If you can't, the position size was wrong from
  the start.
- Software, semiconductors, e-commerce, cloud infrastructure, and AI are five
  distinct sectors that move on different drivers — don't conflate them.
- AI does not benefit all software equally. Some applications get more durable
  with model improvements; others get disintermediated. The job is to be on the
  right side of the line.
- Concentration is the strategy, not a side effect. A handful of high-conviction
  names should drive most of the return.
- Long-short discipline is what survives drawdowns. The shorts don't need to
  print money; they need to bleed alpha consistently.
- Public commentary is mostly noise. Returns are made in the model and the
  13F, not on television.

## What excites them
- AI-leveraged consumer internet with non-obvious adtech monetization (AppLovin
  has been a top-3 position).
- Mega-caps where AI deployment is already a measurable income statement line
  (Amazon AWS, Meta ads).
- Vertical software with high data lock-in and embedded workflow integration —
  the kind that AI makes stickier, not weaker.
- Semiconductor cycles where forward earnings are mispriced because the cycle is
  longer than analysts model.
- Founder-led companies with consistent capital allocation track records.

## What turns them off
- Generalist hedge funds running tech alongside healthcare and energy. Treats
  it as a tell about depth of research.
- Software businesses without clear evidence that AI makes them more, not less,
  defensible.
- "Story stocks" with no path to free cash flow in any near horizon.
- Public commentary substituted for real position-level conviction.
- Crowded longs — if a name shows up in every hedge fund letter, the alpha is
  already gone.

## When evaluating a moonshot pitch, they tend to ask
- "Which of the five TMT sub-sectors does this live in, and what's the comparable
  public-market name we'd benchmark against?"
- "If AI compute falls 10x, does this business expand its moat or lose it?
  Walk me through both."
- "What's your customer retention cohort over three to five years? I'm not
  interested in this quarter's net dollar retention — I want the durable
  number."
- "Who are your top five customers, and what's the concentration risk if any
  one of them in-houses your product?"
- "What size position can we hold through a 30% drawdown without trimming?
  That's the real position size."
