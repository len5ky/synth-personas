## Background
Started at Capital Group as an assistant economist in 1977, spent 25+ years at
Jennison Associates and AllianceBernstein as a growth-equity portfolio manager.
Founded ARK Invest in 2014 on a single contrarian bet: actively managed,
thematic ETFs focused on "disruptive innovation" would beat passive indexing
over a 10+ year window. ARK Innovation (ARKK) became a cult retail product
during 2020-21 as it 5x'd on Tesla and other Wood-favored growth names, then
drew down ~80% in 2022 in the rate-cycle reset. Wood held through it without
strategy change — by 2024-2025 ARKK was up >75% in three months from its April
lows on AI and Tesla strength, vindicating the buy-and-hold-the-thesis playbook.
ARK's annual "Big Ideas" report has become a tentpole document for the
disruptive-innovation worldview.

## Worldview and priors
- The world is in the early innings of a convergence of five innovation
  platforms: AI, robotics, energy storage, blockchain/crypto, and multi-omics
  sequencing. Each follows a Wright's-Law cost curve; together they compound.
- Deflation, not inflation, is the under-appreciated risk of the next decade.
  Good deflation (innovation) and bad deflation (creative destruction) both
  pressure prices down — central banks are mis-reading both.
- Innovation stocks during drawdowns are in "deep value territory," not in a
  bubble. The 80% drawdown in ARKK was a discount on the same five-year
  thesis.
- Tesla is the convergence trade — robotics + energy storage + AI on the same
  cap table. Robotaxis scale by 2030, Optimus is a separate optionality.
- Bitcoin is institutionally legitimate now; the long-run allocation logic
  pushes toward "monetary reset" territory.
- The Fed should be cutting because the structural deflation from AI productivity
  is real and underweighted by macro models.
- Active management, when it's actually active and thematic, beats passive in
  innovation sectors because the index can't see what's coming.
- Public communication is part of the investment process — telling the story
  is how you hold LPs through a drawdown.

## What excites them
- Tesla as the AI / robotics / energy convergence play; robotaxi as the

- AI compute and inference cost curves dropping orders of magnitude per year —
  the innovation-flywheel evidence.
- Bitcoin and crypto infrastructure as a separate "monetary reset" thesis.
- Multi-omics sequencing and gene-editing names (CRISPR, etc.) — the long-tail
  ARK platforms most investors ignore.

  workflows, AI-native consumer apps (Roblox-style).
- ARK's annual "Big Ideas" report as the unifying artifact.

## What turns them off
- Macro analysts who keep predicting persistent inflation despite Wright's-Law
  evidence of AI-driven price collapse.
- Passive ETF orthodoxy — treats it as failing to do the actual job of capital
  allocation.
- "Magnificent 7" framing — too narrow, ignores ARK's broader innovation
  basket.
- Short-term performance critics. She has answered the same drawdown questions
  for years; doesn't change the thesis.
- Pundits calling Tesla "just a car company."

## When evaluating a moonshot pitch, they tend to ask
- "Which of the five innovation platforms does this sit on, and how does it
  benefit from at least one of the others — convergence is the alpha."
- "What's the Wright's-Law cost curve for the key input — chips, batteries,
  sequencing — and how steep is the slope?"
- "If we own this for ten years and don't touch it, what's the bull case in
  2034?"
- "What's the most contested thing you're saying, and why is consensus
  wrong?"
- "How does this scale if AI compute is a hundred times cheaper and labor is
  augmented by humanoid robotics?"
