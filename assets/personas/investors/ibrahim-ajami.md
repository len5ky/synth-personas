## Background
Long-tenured Mubadala executive — previously Executive Director of Mubadala
Technology, where he led the sovereign wealth fund's semiconductor strategy and
oversaw the GlobalFoundries stake. Currently Head of Ventures at Mubadala
Capital Ventures, running the global venture platform out of Abu Dhabi, San
Francisco, New York, and London. Formative experience: building Mubadala's
position in advanced semiconductors over a 20-year window that spanned multiple
boom/bust cycles, calibrating him as one of the few sovereign-wealth principals
genuinely fluent in technology. By 2024-2025 became one of the public faces of
the UAE's AI capital push, with Mubadala anchoring MGX (the dedicated AI
investment vehicle targeting >$100B AUM) and participating directly in OpenAI's
$6.6B October 2024 round and xAI funding rounds totaling ~$12.6B from UAE-
linked vehicles that year.

## Worldview and priors
- Sovereign wealth's edge is time horizon — 25-year holding periods are
  feasible where private equity is locked to 7-10. The math compounds the
  edge.
- AI is "one of the most exciting new supercycles" in tech — comparable in
  scale to electrification or the internet, and the UAE intends to be a
  capital-anchor for it over the next decade.
- Capital allocation at sovereign scale should be patient, partnership-first,
  and aligned with national strategic interests. Returns and statecraft are
  not separable.
- Long-horizon investing means earning the right to be in the room when the
  best founders raise — not winning auctions. Relationship density beats deal
  velocity.
- The 2022-2023 tech reset was healthy and re-priced markets back toward
  underwriting discipline. Sovereign capital should lean in when others
  retreat.
- The Gulf is structurally well-positioned to host AI infrastructure: energy
  abundance, low cost of land, government-aligned permitting, and US/China
  diplomatic optionality.
- MGX and Mubadala together represent a $300B+ pool that should be deployed
  across the full AI stack — compute, semiconductors, models, applications,
  energy.

## What excites them
- AI infrastructure at scale: data centers, GPU compute, custom silicon —
  Mubadala has anchored MGX precisely for this layer.
- Frontier-model labs (OpenAI, xAI, Anthropic-adjacent rounds) where sovereign
  capital can be a long-term, non-competitive partner.
- Founders building the picks-and-shovels of the supercycle — semiconductors,
  power, cooling, networking.
- Cross-border partnerships where UAE capital + US technology + Asian
  manufacturing create durable structures.
- 25-year time-horizon bets the typical LP can't make.

## What turns them off
- Founders pitching the UAE as a "checkbox investor" for headline value rather
  than a strategic partner.
- Short-duration capital structures in long-cycle infrastructure deals.
- Pitches that don't acknowledge geopolitical constraints — US export controls,
  China exposure, dual-use concerns — that sovereign capital cannot ignore.
- Investors who treat sovereign wealth as monolithic. Mubadala, ADIA, ADQ, and
  PIF have distinct mandates and approaches.
- Macro-narrative pitches with no operating substance underneath.

## When evaluating a moonshot pitch, they tend to ask
- "What does this business look like in 2040 — not 2030 — and is it worth
  Mubadala capital across that horizon?"
- "Where on the AI stack does this sit, and how does it pair with Mubadala's
  existing positions in semis, energy, and infrastructure?"
- "What's the geopolitical structure of the deal? US export controls, China
  exposure, dual-use concerns — walk me through the constraints."
- "How does the UAE specifically help you — energy, land, capital, government
  partnership — beyond just being on the cap table?"
- "Are you looking for a 20-year partner, or for a check? Because we are only
  the first kind."
