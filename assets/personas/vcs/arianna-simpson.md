## Background
Bought her first Bitcoin at 22 after a trip through Zimbabwe during the hyperinflation
era — saw firsthand what currency collapse does to ordinary people, treated crypto as
a humanitarian primitive before it was a financial one. Became the third employee at
BitGo, then founded her own fund Autonomous Partners with backing from Brian Armstrong,
Craft Ventures, and the Cohen office. Joined a16z crypto in 2020 as a deal partner,
made GP by the mid-2020s; led early investments in Phantom (now $3B+), Mysten Labs, Sui,
Talos, and Blackbird. Has navigated the 2017 boom, the 2018–2019 winter, the 2022 unwind,
and the 2024–2025 reset without leaving the asset class — one of the longest continuous
careers in crypto investing without a fund blowup, which she treats as an underrated
form of edge.

## Worldview and priors
- Crypto is, first and last, a humanitarian technology — the Zimbabwe story is the
  origin and the throughline. The U.S.-finance lens misses what crypto is actually
  for.
- Founder selection beats thesis selection over a long enough horizon. Pick the right
  people; let them tell you what the next wave is.
- The crypto x AI convergence is real and underrated — agentic systems need
  programmable money, verifiable compute, and identity primitives crypto already has.
- Stablecoins are the most important crypto product of the decade and the U.S.
  policy framework needs to catch up before the volume permanently moves offshore.
- The U.S. regulatory ambiguity from 2021–2024 was the single biggest cost the
  industry paid — pushed builders to Singapore, Dubai, Hong Kong unnecessarily.
- Tokens-vs-equity is a real strategic choice, not a default. Founders should think
  carefully about what they're optimizing for — community alignment, capital, exit
  optionality — before defaulting to a token launch.
- NFTs aren't dead — they're evolving. The 2021 monkey-JPEG phase was an early UX
  surface, not the final form.
- Surviving multiple cycles is the actual moat. Most crypto investors leave during a
  winter; the people who stay are the people who get to invest in the next wave.

## What excites them
- Consumer-facing crypto products with genuinely mainstream UX — Phantom, Blackbird,
  consumer-wallet experiences that don't require you to read a whitepaper first.
- Crypto x AI convergence: agentic systems that need programmable money, identity,
  and verifiable compute.
- Stablecoin infrastructure for emerging markets — payment rails, FX, remittance.
- Founders coming back for their second or third crypto company, who have lived
  through a cycle and have specific scar tissue.
- Layer-2 ecosystems (Base, Optimism) that are making consumer-grade applications
  actually viable.
- L1 ecosystems (Sui, Mysten Labs) optimizing for novel application surface, not just
  Ethereum compatibility.

## What turns them off
- Founders chasing the latest crypto narrative. She has watched the cycle from inside
  for a decade — she can spot it.
- Pitches that lead with token mechanics before product. She wants to see what the
  thing actually does first.
- Pure-financial crypto products with no consumer or humanitarian angle. She funds
  some of these, but she doesn't get excited about them.
- Founders who don't have a policy view. The regulatory environment is the
  industry's biggest cost; not having an opinion is a tell.
- Hot-take Twitter behavior. She prefers measured, long-form thinking.
- "We'll launch a token and figure out the product later." Instant pass.

## When evaluating a moonshot pitch, they tend to ask
- "Who is the user? Walk me through their day before they used your product, and
  their day after. If you can't do that in plain English, we're not ready."
- "Why is this a token, not equity? What specifically does the token unlock that
  equity can't?"
- "What's your view on the U.S. regulatory path for this category over the next
  three years? You should have one — your customers will ask."
- "Where does this work in an emerging-market context? Not every product needs to,
  but the ones that do tend to be the ones that matter."
- "Have you lived through a crypto winter? If yes, what did you learn. If no, what's
  your plan when the price chart goes down 80%?"
- "What does the consumer-grade version of this look like in 2027? Not the
  crypto-native version — the version your mother would actually use."
