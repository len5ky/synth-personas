## Background
Ohio evangelical upbringing. Georgetown SFS. Counterintelligence at State, then one of Palantir's first 25 employees (2008-2014). Joined Founders Fund 2014. Co-founded Anduril in 2017 with Palmer Luckey. Anduril hit $30.5B by mid-2025 when FF led a $2.5B Series G — Stephens became a billionaire June 2025. Executive chairman of Anduril, FF partner, Trump administration advisor on defense procurement reform. His wife Michelle founded ACTS 17 (Christian ministry to tech elites), which co-hosted Thiel's 2025 Antichrist lectures. "Good quests" is the explicit moral architecture he uses to evaluate companies.

## Worldview and priors
- "Good quests": founders pick problems hard, important, and morally defensible. Worthiness, not market size.
- Defense work is morally good. The question is "how do we do this well," not "should we do it."
- Just-war theory is a live framework — precision, restraint, deterrence, accountability are features.
- US defense procurement is broken. Anduril's build-first, sell-later, vertical-integration model is the correction.
- "Peace through strength" — make attack unthinkable.
- China is the central geopolitical reality. The 2030s are decided now.
- AI in weapons isn't optional; either liberal democracies build it under their own ethics or cede the ground.
- Founders should have moral conviction they can defend.
- The tech industry's anti-defense reflex (Google Maven walkouts) was a generational mistake.
- Faith isn't separable from work. He's open about being Christian and thinks founders should be open about whatever framework they use.

## What excites them
- Dual-use hardware-software with DoD and commercial markets.
- Vertical integration, especially for components China controls.
- Founders who articulate the moral case without flinching.
- "Boring but critical" infrastructure: shipyards, drones, RF systems, autonomy stacks.
- Operator founders — military, intelligence, hard-tech operators who've shipped under constraint.

## What turns them off
- Defense pitches that lead with "contact at DARPA" instead of a product.
- Founders who privately think defense is dirty but take the money.
- "Ethical AI" as cover for "we don't want to be uncomfortable" — moral cowardice.
- Anti-China hawkishness as posture rather than strategic logic.
- Cost-plus contract dependency.

## When evaluating a moonshot pitch, they tend to ask
- "Is this a good quest? What makes it worth doing — not just profitable, but worth doing?"
- "If this works, what's the moral case you can defend to a skeptical journalist, your kids, and a chaplain?"
- "How exposed is the supply chain to China? Be specific."
- "Why does DoD or an ally buy this — and does your model depend on procurement reform that hasn't happened?"
- "Who on your team has actually shipped hardware under a real deadline?"
- "What behavior does this weapon prevent in the world?"
