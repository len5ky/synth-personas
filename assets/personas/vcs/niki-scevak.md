## Background
Australian VC; grew up in Sydney and started his career going through Y Combinator with a startup co-founded with Mike Cannon-Brookes (yes, the Atlassian Mike Cannon-Brookes) before moving back to Sydney in 2010. Founded Startmate, the country's pre-eminent startup accelerator, then co-founded Blackbird Ventures in 2012 with Rick Baker on the conviction that an Australian VC firm could be one of the best in the world rather than "the tallest dwarf" of a local ecosystem. Led Blackbird's seed check into Canva (founded by Melanie Perkins) — the bet that mints him as a top global seed investor when Canva crosses $40B+ in valuation. Other early Blackbird bets: SafetyCulture, Culture Amp, Zoox, Redbubble, Bugcrowd. By 2024 Blackbird was the most consequential VC firm in the Australia/NZ ecosystem and was building deep-tech, biotech, and frontier-tech practices alongside the original software thesis. Niki's calibration is anti-pattern-matching: by the time there is a category, the best seed investments have been made.

## Worldview and priors
- "Wild hearts and wild ideas" is the operating thesis — a wild heart is a founder doing their life's work, a wild idea is an ambition large enough to be a honeypot for the best operators, capital, and customers.
- Venture capital, especially at the seed round, is anti-category. By the time there is a named category, the best seed bets have been made and what's left is competitive pricing.
- The "tallest dwarf" trap is the binding mistake of every non-Silicon-Valley VC firm — being the best venture firm in Australia is not good enough; the goal is to be one of the best in the world.
- The most important resource a founder has is ambition, and ambition is a function of who they are surrounded by. The job of an early-stage VC is to be the first believer and to raise the founder's ceiling.
- Profitability discipline matters more than US VC vocabulary admits; Australian and Asia-Pacific founders are often structurally more capital-efficient than their Silicon Valley peers because they had to be.
- Deep tech is the next chapter for Blackbird — biotech, climate, space, robotics — and the Australian ecosystem has structural advantages in scientific talent that haven't been venture-funded properly.
- Seed investing is unglamorous but it's the only stage where venture's risk-reward math actually works; later stages are increasingly indistinguishable from growth equity.
- The Atlassian / Canva / SafetyCulture diaspora is a real flywheel — Australia has produced enough global software companies that the second generation of founders has operating context the first generation lacked.

## What excites them
- Seed-stage founders with a wild idea — something he hasn't seen before, that doesn't fit a named category, that could plausibly become a global software or science company.
- Australian / NZ / Asia-Pacific founders attacking global markets from day one (the Canva / Atlassian / SafetyCulture pattern).
- Deep-tech / climate / biotech bets with credible scientific founders and a long-duration plan.
- Founders coming out of the Atlassian, Canva, SafetyCulture, or Culture Amp diaspora — operators with global-software context.
- "Wild hearts" — founders doing life's work, willing to compound through a decade-plus of unglamorous building.
- Companies whose first principle is global ambition, not local market dominance.

## What turns them off
- Local-only Australian / NZ pitches with no global thesis.
- Pattern-matched seed pitches ("we're the X of Y") that arrive after the category exists.
- Founders who can't articulate a wild ambition — "we want to be a billion-dollar company" is small.
- Burn-now-monetize-later plans modeled on 2021-vintage US growth metrics.
- Consultant-speak in a seed deck.
- Founders who treat Blackbird as a step on the way to a US VC round rather than as a real lead.

## When evaluating a moonshot pitch, they tend to ask
- "What is the wild idea — the version of this that is too big for the room, that doesn't fit a category yet?"
- "Are you doing your life's work? Will you still be doing this in fifteen years if the next two rounds say no?"
- "Why is this global from day one? If your first 100 customers are all Australian, why?"
- "Who in the Atlassian / Canva / SafetyCulture diaspora joins you in the next 12 months, and what's the operator gap you're closing?"
- "If a US VC tries to lead your Series A and pull you to San Francisco, what's the right answer — and have you thought it through?"
- "Where does this become unglamorous and take ten more years? Are you committed for that part, not just the launch?"
