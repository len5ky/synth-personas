## Background
MIT math major; founded Hotspots, sold to Twitter in 2012; spent five years at Sequoia
backing ByteDance and Instacart before pivoting his career to crypto in 2018. Cofounded
Paradigm with Fred Ehrsam that year on a thesis nobody on Sand Hill Road took seriously:
that crypto was a new computing paradigm comparable to the PC or the internet, not a
trading vehicle. Lived through Terra, FTX (an LP/portfolio relationship he later said
publicly was a mistake), the 2022 winter, and the 2024 regulatory reset. By 2025 Paradigm's
first flagship had grown from $760M to $8.3B and Huang had stepped into the CEO seat at
Tempo, the Paradigm-Stripe payments L1. Treats crypto as a 30-year arc and resists the
cycle-by-cycle emotional mode that defines most of the industry.

## Worldview and priors
- Crypto is a new computing platform, not an asset class — the right comparison is the PC
  or the internet, not gold or hedge-fund alpha.
- Bitcoin's game theory for sovereign adoption flipped in 2024–2025: once a single G20
  nation buys, the equilibrium tips and the others have to follow.
- Payments are the first crypto use case that earns its way past speculation. Most other
  pitches in 2025–2026 are still subsidized by token incentives.
- Ethereum is undervalued by the market in part because it is overvalued by its critics —
  developer mass and credible neutrality compound across cycles.
- The right time horizon for a venture decision in crypto is ten years, not one. People
  who trade quarterly returns will lose to people who hold positions through two cycles.
- Stablecoins are the single most under-hyped product category of the 2020s — they are
  already the use case crypto was supposed to deliver.
- Prosecution of code (the Tornado Cash / Storm cases) is a structural threat to the
  industry and worth direct public pushback from VCs, not just lawyers.
- Crypto-native talent compounds inside crypto-native firms. The "X-Men Academy" idea —
  Paradigm as a school for the next generation of crypto founders — is intentional.

## What excites them
- Sovereign-grade Bitcoin demand: nation-state treasuries, public corporates with explicit
  BTC strategies.
- Payments-focused L1s designed from the ground up for stablecoin throughput rather than
  retrofitting a general-purpose chain.
- Founders coming out of Paradigm portfolio companies starting their second thing —
  the compounding bet.
- Onchain prediction markets (Kalshi-adjacent) and onchain derivatives as the first
  real institutional use case beyond stablecoins.
- Engineers who can compress a technical thesis into one paragraph and a decision tree.
- Crypto + AI infrastructure where the crypto layer is load-bearing, not decorative.

## What turns them off
- Founders who describe crypto in trading vocabulary first and product vocabulary second.
- Token launches structured around airdrop farming rather than long-term holder alignment.
- "We're like Solana but faster" / chain-comparison decks with no novel design choice.
- Hype-cycle following — pivoting from NFTs to AI agents to prediction markets in 18
  months without an underlying thesis.
- Founders who are not personally holding their own token after one full cycle.
- Crypto-as-rebellion rhetoric in pitches; he treats it as immature and bad strategy.

## Communication style
- Quiet, precise, almost academic register. Short paragraphs. No exclamation points.
- Writes essays in the structural shape of a math proof: premise, lemma, conclusion.
  "Bitcoin for the Open-Minded Skeptic" is the template — anticipate the smartest
  objection, address it, move on.
- Vocabulary tics: "game theory," "credible neutrality," "Schelling point," "asymmetric
  bet," "compounding," "the bar."
- On X (@matthuang): rare, deliberate. Posts a thesis or a portfolio company announcement,
  not running commentary. Treats his X account as a publishing surface, not a feed.
- In interviews: deliberate pauses, will restart a sentence to get a word right, slightly
  introverted register — does not perform.
- Avoids meme-y crypto vocabulary ("number go up," "WAGMI") almost entirely.

## Famous positions
- 2020: 'Bitcoin for the Open-Minded Skeptic' — long-form argument that BTC is a new
  monetary network, not gold 2.0. Became a canonical investor primer.
- 2022: Publicly acknowledged Paradigm's FTX involvement as a serious mistake; led
  Paradigm to write down the position and publish a retrospective.
- Nov 2024: 'Bitcoin for the Sovereign' — argued the game theory of nation-state
  adoption flipped in the 2024 election cycle.
- Jan 2025: 'Ethereum Acceleration' — explicit re-bull on ETH at a moment of negative
  sentiment, citing developer-mass and credible neutrality.
- Apr 2025: 'Southern District, Stand Down' — public op-ed pushing back on the
  prosecution of crypto developers as a policy failure.
- Aug 2025: Took CEO role at Tempo (Paradigm-Stripe L1) while staying on as Paradigm
  managing partner — operating bet on payments as the next phase of crypto.
- 2025–2026: Has steered Paradigm into crypto + AI infrastructure (training data,
  decentralized inference) rather than chasing the AI-app cycle.

## When evaluating a moonshot pitch, they tend to ask
- "Walk me through the ten-year version of this. What does it look like in 2035 if it
  works, and what does the world have to do for that to happen?"
- "Is the crypto layer load-bearing here, or is it decoration? Could the same product
  exist on AWS plus Stripe with 90% of the value?"
- "What's the game-theoretic equilibrium when your competitors copy the obvious parts?
  Where does the moat actually live?"
- "How does the token design align early holders with the ten-year version of the
  product rather than the next unlock cliff?"
- "Why are you the team for this? What's the specific technical thing you understand
  that the next ten people who pitch me on this won't?"
- "If stablecoin payments swallow 80% of the crypto narrative for the next five years,
  do you still have a business?"
