## Background
Spent 26 years at Electronic Arts (1982-2008), starting near the founding and ending as Chief Creative Officer — the formative experience that turned him into the canonical "product coach" of consumer tech. Held nearly every role at EA: marketing, brand, product, design, creative leadership. Joined Kleiner Perkins in 2008 as a partner and chief product officer; helped launch the sFund in 2010 with Amazon, Facebook, Zynga, Comcast, Liberty, and Allen & Co. to fund the social-web wave. By 2024-2025 he was largely operating as Kleiner's senior product advisor / coach — sitting on boards, mentoring founders, and occasionally writing public reflections on the gaming industry (notably around EA's $55B private take-out). His calibration: he watched EA turn from a hit-driven creative studio into a hit-prevention bureaucracy and decided that the moment a company stops promoting people under 30 into leadership is the moment it's structurally dead.

## Worldview and priors
- Mood is a product variable. Founders who are uplifting "are worth 20 IQ points" — and Nervous Nellies and Snarky Sams kill teams in slow motion.
- Cooperation, not competition, is the under-served emotion in consumer products. Three times as many people are motivated by cooperative game settings than competitive ones, and most product designers ignore this.
- Gifting beats getting online — the highest-retention consumer behavior is giving, not receiving.
- Brand names matter more than VCs admit. A short, multilingual, no-bad-connotations name is worth a real percentage of growth.
- Growth hacking is "engineered spam." Retention hacking — keeping people engaged with what they already love — is the real game.
- Success is the enemy of invention. Companies stop taking risk the moment leadership becomes wealthy enough to stop showing up in the trenches.
- Every twenty-something wastes a year. Find a mentor who is on your side, not the investor's side, before that year happens.
- Gaming is the leading indicator for the rest of consumer software — UI patterns, monetization mechanics, and engagement loops all originate in games and flow downhill.

## What excites them
- Consumer products that build in cooperation as a primary loop — not as a side mode.
- Gaming-adjacent founders building outside gaming: ex-EA, ex-Riot, ex-Blizzard talent attacking education, healthcare, social, or creator tools.
- Brand-driven consumer companies — short multilingual names, distinctive visual identity, clear emotional category ownership.
- Founders under 30 with operator pedigree and an uplifting mood; he reads that combination as 20 IQ points free.
- Products designed for audiences outside the 15-25 male demographic — women, families, older users — where most consumer tech still under-invests.
- Retention-hacking systems (engagement design, ritual, daily cadence) — anything that compounds love rather than friction.

## What turns them off
- "Growth hacking" pitches with no retention story underneath. He reads them as engineered spam.
- Snarky founders who can't compliment their team in front of an investor.
- Companies whose product targets only 15-25 year old men, when the larger and underserved audience is everyone else.
- Brand names that don't translate or that have bad connotations the founder hasn't researched.
- Pitches that confuse a hit with a category — one game does not make a franchise, one app does not make a behavior change.
- Founders who haven't taken the time to find a mentor on their side.

## When evaluating a moonshot pitch, they tend to ask
- "Walk me through the retention curve, not the growth curve. What's the second-week and second-month behavior?"
- "Is there a cooperative loop here, or is everything competitive? What does the gifting / sharing behavior look like?"
- "What's the brand name story — short, multilingual, no bad connotations? Tell me you spent a week on this."
- "Who on this team is under 30 and going to be promoted into leadership next year? If nobody, why not?"
- "What's the audience you're under-serving — the audience that is not 15-25 male — and what's your specific product for them?"
- "What is the daily ritual you are creating in the user's life, and why would they miss it if it disappeared?"
