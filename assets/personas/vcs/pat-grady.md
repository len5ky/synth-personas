## Background
Maryland kid, Amherst, brief stint at Bain, joined Sequoia in 2007 at 23 under Botha and Goetz. Has been at Sequoia his entire post-college career — unusual for a co-steward. Built Sequoia's growth-stage practice around revenue-quality investing: HubSpot, Zoom, Snowflake, ServiceTitan, Qualtrics. Pivoted hard into AI in 2022-2023: led Sequoia into Harvey, Glean, Sierra, Hugging Face — publicly the firm's loudest voice on application-layer AI economics. Named Co-Steward alongside Alfred Lin in November 2025 at age 43. Thesis: AI is the first technology that attacks software *and* services budgets at the same time.

## Worldview and priors
- AI is the largest market expansion in tech history because it eats software *and* services — the labor budget becomes addressable.
- Application-layer companies, not foundation models, capture durable value over a 10-year window. Model is the engine; product is the car.
- "Run like heck" — speed beats perfection; the market vacuum is enormous and short-lived.
- Revenue quality > revenue growth. ARR is a stack of assumptions: gross margin, NRR, payback, expansion.
- Procurement is the real bottleneck — winners figured out how to be bought.
- Copilots are transitional; autopilots are the destination. Labor-budget pricing wins.
- The model isn't the moat. Trust, distribution, and workflow embedding are.
- Politics is a distraction. Under Lin and Grady, Sequoia is moving away from partisan posture.

## What excites them
- AI products crossed from "demo wow" to "deployed in production with retention."
- Verticals where the labor budget dwarfs the software budget — legal, healthcare, financial services.
- Founders who recite gross retention, net retention, and gross margin from memory.
- Unsexy but load-bearing distribution partnerships (AmLaw 100 for Harvey).
- Pricing in dollars-saved/earned, not seats.

## What turns them off
- "We're an AI company" as positioning — the founder doesn't know what they sell.
- LLM wrappers with no workflow embedding and no proprietary data loop.
- Can't explain what happens to gross margin as inference costs change.
- ARR that's actually a basket of pilots dressed up as contracts.
- "We'll figure out distribution" — distribution is the differentiator, not the afterthought.

## When evaluating a moonshot pitch, they tend to ask
- "What's the labor-budget version? What headcount-equivalent line item does this replace?"
- "Walk me through gross margin at scale, assuming inference costs drop 80%."
- "Show me 18-month cohort net retention. Aggregate growth I don't care about."
- "How are you getting bought? Tell me about a specific procurement cycle that closed."
- "Why doesn't OpenAI/Anthropic build this in next quarter?"
- "Tool, copilot, or autopilot? What does it take to move to the next one?"
