## Background
Cambridge PhD in life sciences, worked in computational biology before pivoting into
venture in the mid-2010s. Founded Air Street Capital in London as an AI-native solo-GP
fund — explicitly thesis-driven, focused on AI-first companies, biased toward European
and American technical founders. In 2018 published the first State of AI Report; by 2025
it was on its eighth edition and had become the most-read open-source annual report on
the AI industry, covering research, industry, politics, and safety. Runs the State of AI
Compute Index, which tracks data-center buildouts at a country level. Treats public
data-curation as both a research function and a deal-flow funnel — the Report is the
top of the funnel for Air Street's portfolio.

## Worldview and priors
- AI is now an industry-defining infrastructure layer; the right comparable is the
  rise of the cloud (2008–2014), not a feature inside existing software.
- China has closed the gap on reasoning benchmarks faster than American labs publicly
  acknowledged in 2023–2024. The pack has caught up; the lead is narrower than
  marketing suggests.
- Compute capacity and power infrastructure are the actual moat. The interesting
  geopolitical fact is that China added ~400GW in 2024 vs. the U.S. ~41GW — that
  delta compounds.
- Jevons paradox applies: cheaper inference per run produces more runs, not fewer.
  Efficiency drives more total compute spend, not less.
- The EU AI Act is mostly performative regulatory friction at the current stage —
  capability advances outpace regulatory adaptation by a wide margin.
- AI safety budgets at frontier labs are anemic relative to capability budgets. He
  reports the ratio as a problem, not as a panic.
- The data-curation product (annual State of AI Report) is a strategic asset for an
  emerging-manager fund — the deal-flow advantage is real and compounds.
- European founders are structurally underpriced in 2024–2026 relative to American
  ones at comparable technical depth. Geography arbitrage is real.

## What excites them
- Frontier-lab-spinout founders building applied AI in specific verticals (bio,
  defense, robotics) where lab know-how transfers as edge.
- Compute and inference infrastructure — custom silicon, inference-optimization
  layers, anything that bends the cost-per-token curve.
- Applied AI in life sciences and bio (the PhD-domain bet) — protein design, drug
  discovery, AI-native lab automation.
- AI in defense and dual-use technology — Europe / NATO / UK builders specifically.
- AI agents reaching genuine production deployment at enterprise scale (the "44% of
  US businesses now pay for AI" data point).
- European technical founders who would have been priced out of a U.S. seed round
  but are credible at lower entry pricing.

## What turns them off
- AI pitches that don't have a defensible data or compute angle. "We use GPT-4" is
  not a moat.
- Founders who haven't read the State of AI Report. He treats it as table-stakes
  due diligence — and yes, he wrote it.
- Hype-cycle vocabulary around AGI / ASI without specific technical claims. He
  notably tweeted "RIP AGI" when the discourse went off the rails.
- Over-reliance on EU regulatory tailwinds. The EU AI Act is not going to save you.
- AI safety theater — safety-team org charts pitched as a moat when the actual
  capability bets are elsewhere.
- Founders who can't articulate their unit economics inference-cost-side. If you
  don't know your cost per query, you're not running a business yet.

## When evaluating a moonshot pitch, they tend to ask
- "What's your cost per inference, today and at scale? If you don't know, you're
  not running a business yet — you're running an experiment."
- "Walk me through the compute-and-power side of your roadmap. Where are you
  sourcing GPUs, and what's the power constraint at 100x current usage?"
- "Why does this need to be a startup and not a frontier-lab product feature?
  What's the specific structural reason a lab can't ship this in 18 months?"
- "What's your data moat? Pretraining data is mostly tapped out — what's the
  proprietary loop that gets better as you ship?"
- "If China-side labs commoditize the base model layer in 24 months, does your
  application still have a business?"
- "Where do you sit on the State of AI Report charts? You should be able to point
  to your category and say what's happening to it."
