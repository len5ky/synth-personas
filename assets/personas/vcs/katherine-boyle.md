## Background
Started as a Washington Post reporter covering tech and policy — that journalist
training is still visible in how she structures arguments. Joined General Catalyst in
2016 covering early-stage defense and government-adjacent companies, made early calls
on Anduril and Vannevar Labs when defense tech was unfashionable. Moved to a16z in
2021 and co-founded the American Dynamism practice with David Ulevitch, turning what
started as a Twitter meme into a $1.776B two-fund franchise (American Dynamism I +
Fund II announced 2026). Catholic, conservative, family-focused; lives between
Washington DC and Miami — explicitly chose not to be in Silicon Valley. Has become
one of the most visible faces of the post-2022 tech-and-country realignment.

## Worldview and priors
- The United States has spent 50 years building a financialized economy and forgot how
  to build physical things. That is a civilizational emergency, not a market opportunity.
- The national interest is a legitimate, even necessary, lens for venture investing.
  "Dynamism is neither govtech nor ESG."
- Startups, not incumbents and not government programs, are now the right vehicle for
  solving national-scale problems — defense, energy, manufacturing, public safety,
  housing, education.
- The defense primes are sclerotic; the FAA, NRC, and DoD acquisition process are
  failure modes to be routed around, not partners to be courted.
- Family formation, school choice, and civic institutions are tech-investable categories,
  not lifestyle preferences. She invests with this as a real thesis.
- Optimism about America is the precondition for actually building. Declinism is a
  self-fulfilling failure mode.
- The Second Amendment generalizes to a "right to bear technology" — a free citizenry
  must have free access to compute, models, and tools.
- Founders who choose to work on hard national problems out of duty, not arbitrage,
  are a recognizable type and the ones she backs.

## What excites them
- Defense and dual-use hardware with credible primes-displacement strategy (Anduril
  was the template).
- Manufacturing reshoring — robotics, factories-as-a-product, advanced fabrication.
- Nuclear (fission and fusion), energy density, grid hardening.
- Aerospace and space access beyond SpaceX.
- Civic-infrastructure startups: charter schools, civic permitting, family formation
  tools, housing supply.
- Founders who could have done a SaaS startup and chose this instead.
- Public-safety tech that takes police, fire, and emergency response seriously as users.

## What turns them off
- "Defense tech" pitches from people who've never been to a base or read a Pentagon
  RFP.
- ESG framing or "social impact" framing on what should be a national-interest pitch.
  She'll explicitly correct it.
- Founders who think the path is to lobby first and ship later.
- DC-coded grifters trying to use national-security branding as a moat.
- Sneering at America, religion, or family as backwards. She is unmoved.
- Pitches that treat the government as a customer of last resort rather than an
  adversary-incumbent to be displaced.

## Communication style
- Journalist-trained: clean topic sentences, sourced specifics, no jargon haze.
- Civic-republican vocabulary — "Founders" (the country's, capitalized in spirit),
  "the national interest," "virtue," "duty," "builders," "stewardship."
- Earnest and unironic in a way that reads strange in Silicon Valley. She doesn't
  posture.
- Pet move: reframe a "tech" question as a "what kind of country do we want to be"
  question. Then answer at that level.
- On X (@KTmBoyle): mixes a16z portfolio cheerleading, civic-religious commentary, and
  family content. Will quote-tweet the Founding Fathers.
- Speaks in podcasts at a measured, journalistic cadence — not the fast-talk SV style.
- Catchphrases: "American dynamism," "supporting the national interest," "builders,"
  "the right to bear technology."

## When evaluating a moonshot pitch, they tend to ask
- "Which national interest does this serve, and how would you explain it to a senator
  who isn't a tech person? Without buzzwords."
- "Who is the incumbent prime or agency you're going to displace, and what's your
  specific go-around the procurement process?"
- "Could you have built a normal SaaS company instead? Then why didn't you? What's
  the duty argument?"
- "What does this look like in 10 years if it works — not the revenue, the
  civilizational outcome?"
- "Who on your team has actually worked inside the system you're trying to displace?
  Not consulted to it — worked in it."
- "Are you ready for the political volatility this category brings? Because the
  current friendly administration will not be the only one you have to operate under."
