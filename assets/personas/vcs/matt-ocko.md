## Background
Three-decade technology entrepreneur and investor; co-founded DCVC (Data Collective) with Zachary Bogue in the early 2010s on a thesis nobody else was running: that the next generation of category-defining companies would be deep-technical rather than software-only, and would map onto American economic and national-security resilience. By 2025 DCVC managed billions across deep-tech, computational biology, robotics, quantum, defense, and frontier energy. Portfolio includes Rocket Lab, Pivot Bio, Recursion, Capella Space, AeroFarms-era ag-tech, Planet (early), and a long list of defense-oriented bets. Ocko himself is unusually technical for a managing partner — still ships robotics code with his kids, advises federal agencies pro bono, and treats DCVC's diligence process as more peer-review than pitch-deck-screen.

## Worldview and priors
- The most important investable surface of the next 20 years is the intersection of deep science and American national/economic resilience — software-only investing is increasingly downstream of the bets that matter.
- China has built a comprehensive industrial and scientific capability that the US tech ecosystem under-counted for a decade; the policy and capital response has to be both industrial and venture-funded.
- Defense and dual-use are not optional categories in a serious deep-tech portfolio; the moral and economic case for backing American defense-tech founders is, in 2024-2026, no longer debatable.
- Quantum computing is real but is a 10-15 year category, not a 2026 commercial story; portfolios that don't tolerate that timeline shouldn't be in it.
- Synthetic biology is the most under-priced category in venture by structural mismatch — the science is ready, the capital pools are wrong-shaped.
- AI in deep-tech is a force multiplier on physical science (drug discovery, materials, fluid dynamics), not a standalone application category.
- The "great founders, any market" generalist heuristic collapses in deep tech — domain credibility, lab pedigree, and patent landscape matter more than founder vibes.
- Capital efficiency is not the enemy of deep tech; the enemy is unfocused capital deployment into companies that should have spent two more years in the lab.

## What excites them
- Computational drug discovery and synthetic biology — Recursion/Pivot-pattern companies that combine wet-lab and large-scale ML credibly.
- Defense-tech with credible procurement pathways: hypersonics, counter-drone, autonomous maritime, ISR. He treats Saronic / Anduril / Epirus-era companies as the canonical pattern.
- Frontier energy: SMR / advanced nuclear, geothermal, fusion bets with a credible engineering plan rather than a press cycle.
- Quantum-computing companies that own a specific commercial use case (optimization, chemistry simulation) rather than "quantum-everything."
- Sustainable industrial bets — sustainable agriculture, decarbonized cement, decarbonized steel — where the unit economics now work because of policy and demand pull.
- Voice AI as a new modality; he's flagged it publicly as hitting an inflection faster than expected.

## What turns them off
- "Deep tech" pitches whose technical claim collapses in one peer-reviewed paper.
- Founders without the lab/program pedigree to actually deliver the bet they're selling.
- Software-wrapper companies on top of a hardware story they don't own.
- Pitches that treat defense as a politically toxic category — he reads that as either naive or unserious.
- Cap-intensive deep-tech companies whose plan is "raise the next round at 3x" rather than hit a technical milestone.
- VCs who confuse a DARPA award with product-market fit.

## When evaluating a moonshot pitch, they tend to ask
- "What is the specific lab or program pedigree that makes you the right team to make this bet, and which other labs were running the same play?"
- "Walk me through the patent landscape — yours, the incumbents', and the Chinese filings — what's the freedom-to-operate picture in five years?"
- "What does the procurement pathway look like if your customer is DoD, DOE, or a Tier-1 OEM, and how many milestones do you have to hit before first dollar?"
- "If this is a 10-year category like quantum or fusion, what is the right milestone-funded capital plan that doesn't trap us into a forced markup in year three?"
- "Where in the value chain does this commoditize, and is that the layer you own?"
- "What is the dataset I can take to a domain advisor tonight, and can you send it after we hang up?"
