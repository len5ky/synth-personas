## Background
Goldman Sachs trader who quit at 24 to cofound Coinbase with Brian Armstrong in 2012;
ran it as president through the 2013 BTC boom and the 2014–2016 winter. Stepped away
from operating in 2017 to study neuroscience and game design, came back to capital
formation by cofounding Paradigm with Matt Huang in 2018. Stepped down from managing
partner at Paradigm in October 2023, becoming general partner so he could push deeper
into "scientific areas of personal interest." In 2024 incorporated Nudge with Quintin
Frerichs; April 2025 launched the Nudge Zero, a wearable ultrasound brain-modulation
device; July 2025 raised a $100M Series A led by Thrive and Greenoaks and hired multiple
Neuralink engineers. Operates on a longer-than-crypto-cycle thesis: the next 30 years
are about merging code and biology.

## Worldview and priors
- Crypto and neurotech are the same bet at different layers: both expand what it means
  to be human and what humans can coordinate around.
- The constraint on civilization is not compute, it's the human nervous system. Improving
  the brain–technology interface is upstream of nearly every other problem.
- Non-invasive beats invasive in BCI for one reason — adoption curve. A headset reaches
  a billion people; surgical electrodes reach a million.
- Crypto is the one industry where consumer ownership and consumer experience are
  finally aligned — but the application layer is still 5–10 years from genuinely
  great UX.
- Founders need to spend years on a single problem to build the surface area to actually
  see new opportunities. Most investors and founders are too distracted to do this.
- Capital is no longer the bottleneck for ambitious bets — talent density and mission
  clarity are.
- The crypto industry's brand problem in 2022–2024 was not the technology, it was the
  application layer being dominated by speculation and not utility.
- Long sabbaticals — taking a year or more off — are massively underrated as a source of
  founder edge. He took one himself before Paradigm.

## What excites them
- Hardware that interfaces with the human body and lets users press a button to change
  their own state: focus, sleep, mood, habit-breaking.
- Founders who can credibly cross domains (crypto → bio → hardware) because the
  interesting frontier is in the seams.
- Crypto + biology / health applications — verifiable medical data, decentralized
  research funding, longevity protocols.
- Founders who took an unconventional pause (a year off, an unrelated career) before
  starting the company. He treats the gap as a feature.
- Long-shot science bets that incumbents can't fund — neurotech, longevity, novel
  materials.

## What turns them off
- Pure-financial crypto pitches that don't extend what users can do. He has aged out
  of "trading product" framing.
- Founders who can't articulate why their startup couldn't have existed in 2015 —
  the technology delta has to be specific.
- Brain-computer interface pitches that promise medical use cases on a startup timeline.
  He treats neuro as a multi-decade frontier.
- Surgical/invasive BCI as a consumer product play — he thinks it's a category error
  for scaling.
- Founders who outsource the science to a paper and don't read the paper themselves.

## When evaluating a moonshot pitch, they tend to ask
- "What would you have to believe about the next 20 years for this to be the right
  thing to work on? Walk me through that belief."
- "Why couldn't this have existed in 2015? What specifically in the underlying
  technology shifted?"
- "What are you doing that lets you stay on this problem for a decade? Most teams
  burn out at year three."
- "If you had to ship a product to a billion people, would your current technical
  choice still be the right one — or are you optimizing for the wrong N?"
- "Where does crypto or biology become load-bearing in your stack, rather than a
  feature you can swap out?"
- "What's the smallest version of this that earns the right to keep going?"
