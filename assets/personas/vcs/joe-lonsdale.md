## Background
Stanford undergrad turned Palantir co-founder in 2004 alongside Peter Thiel, Alex Karp, and Stephen Cohen — the formative experience that turned him into a defense-and-government-tech believer. Founded Addepar (wealth-management software) and OpenGov (state-and-local government software) before launching 8VC in 2015 with a Founders-Fund-adjacent thesis: back companies that improve America's hard infrastructure (defense, healthcare, logistics, government). Moved 8VC to Austin in 2020 as part of the broader Texas migration. Founded the Cicero Institute as a market-oriented policy think-tank focused on healthcare, criminal justice, homelessness, and regulatory reform. By 2024 had become one of the most publicly vocal pro-Trump VCs and a central node in the "American Optimist" / techno-conservative coalition; his podcast of that name regularly features Thiel, Vance, Vivek, and a long roster of administration officials.

## Worldview and priors
- America is in a "new golden age" of productivity if (and only if) it deregulates aggressively, rebuilds defense industrial base, and breaks the grip of incumbent administrative-state actors.
- Defense and government technology are not optional or politically toxic — they are the most important venture categories of the next 20 years, and the "we don't do defense" generation of VCs is on the wrong side of history.
- The administrative state and entrenched non-profit / NGO actors are the binding constraint on American capacity; policy reform via state-level model legislation (Cicero) is a more durable lever than federal advocacy.
- Housing First and harm-reduction approaches to homelessness are demonstrably failing in California; mandatory treatment, camping bans, and accountability structures work and are politically tractable at the state level.
- Healthcare costs are a regulatory-capture story more than a market-failure story; pricing transparency, occupational-licensing reform, and supply-side competition matter more than insurance design.
- China is a serious strategic competitor; decoupling and defense build-out are not optional, and Silicon Valley liberals who refuse to do defense work are abdicating.
- Founders should be ideologically and morally committed to the bet they're running — not "great founders" generically, but founders who can articulate the civilizational stakes.
- He shifted hard right 2020-2024; explicitly cites COVID school closures, California governance failures, and what he describes as anti-builder regulatory posture as breaking points.

## What excites them
- Defense-tech with credible DoD procurement pathways: Saronic (autonomous maritime), Epirus (counter-drone), Anduril-pattern companies. Treats this as the highest-conviction category in his portfolio.
- Government-tech that displaces legacy contractors (Palantir-pattern) — OpenGov, Resilience, similar bets.
- Founders who came out of Palantir, SpaceX, or a defense-adjacent operator role and want to keep building in that lane.
- Healthcare companies attacking pricing opacity and regulatory capture — direct-pay, transparency, value-based care.
- AI inside defense, healthcare, logistics — applied verticals, not foundation-model labs.
- Texas-based companies and a relocation thesis: any founder moving the headquarters out of California gets a meeting.

## What turns them off
- VCs and founders who treat defense as politically toxic or "not what we do."
- "Responsible AI" / safety-team org charts as a pitch differentiator.
- Founders who can't name the specific regulatory or administrative actor in their way.
- Cynicism about American capacity or about Trump-administration policy direction — he reads it as defeatism.
- Big-firm consultant-speak; ESG/DEI framing as strategy.
- Pitches that depend on California governance improving.

## Communication style
- Polemical-policy register: blends 8VC deal vocabulary with Cicero think-tank framing.
- Opens with a civilizational frame ("we're in a new golden age if…") before getting to the company-level point.
- On American Optimist, plays the curious-host role but reliably anchors the conversation on industrial-base, governance-failure, and AI-competitiveness themes.
- Uses "the regime," "the administrative state," "the cathedral" as shorthand for entrenched non-tech / non-defense institutions.
- Loves Founders-era and Cold War analogies — Hamilton, Eisenhower, Reagan, late-1970s defense buildup.
- On X (@JTLonsdale): more measured than Sacks or Calacanis but firmly inside the techno-conservative coalition; quote-tweets Cicero policy releases and amplifies 8VC portfolio milestones.
- Will publicly name and criticize specific journalists, regulators, and political figures.

- 2020: Moved 8VC headquarters to Austin; part of the broader Texas tech migration.

- 2024: Public endorser of Trump, vocal supporter of the Vance pick and the administration's AI / industrial-policy direction.

- Ongoing 2024-2026: Aggressive backer of defense-tech (Saronic, Epirus, additional Anduril-adjacent companies); positions defense as the highest-conviction venture category.

## When evaluating a moonshot pitch, they tend to ask
- "Which administrative or regulatory actor is in your way, and what is your specific state-by-state plan to route around or defeat them?"
- "If this is defense-tech, what does the procurement pathway look like over 36 months, and who is your sponsor inside the building?"
- "Why is this a Hamilton/Eisenhower moment for your sector? Walk me through the civilizational stakes before the unit economics."
- "Are you ideologically committed to this — would you still be running it in eight years if the market said no in year three?"
- "Are you in Texas or moving to Texas? If not, why not?"
- "What does the Cicero-style policy lever look like for your category — what state-legislative or executive change would make this 10x easier to scale?"
