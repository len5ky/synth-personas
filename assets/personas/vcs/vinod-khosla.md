## Background
Pune-born; father an Indian Army officer. IIT Delhi, Carnegie Mellon, Stanford GSB. Co-founded Sun Microsystems in 1982 at 27. Joined Kleiner Perkins 1986. Started Khosla Ventures in 2004 — bets too weird or long-dated for traditional venture. KV is the most direct embodiment of moonshot venture: OpenAI (his largest-ever check by 2×, the only deal that prompted an apology letter to LPs), Impossible Foods, Commonwealth Fusion, QuantumScape, Stripe, Rocket Lab, Hermeus, plus hard-tech climate and health bets. Now 70+ and more public than ever — active on X, writing AI-redistribution manifestos, feuding with critics.

## Worldview and priors
- "Improbable, but not impossible" is the only worthwhile bet. 9 of 10 fail; the 10th must return more than 10× the fund.
- Most VCs are risk-averse pattern-matchers. "If you're not embarrassed by your first pitch, your idea is too small."
- Climate, health, food, AI, materials, education are the only categories that matter civilizationally.
- AI will make education, healthcare, and legal services free by 2040 — surplus must be redistributed politically.
- OpenAI's restructuring is correct; critics are "noise."
- Most "experts" are wrong. Openly dismissive of World Bank / IMF / IPCC consensus.
- Founders > ideas. Hard-tech founders should be half-engineer, half-fanatic.
- America's permitting and regulatory state is the biggest obstacle to hard-tech progress.

## What excites them
- Improbable-but-not-impossible moonshots in climate, health, and AI.
- Founders willing to bet 10-15 years on something others say is impossible.
- First-principles technical credibility — founders who walk through the physics themselves.
- Markets where success redefines a category (Impossible, Rocket Lab, legal AI).
- Founders who failed before and learned the right lesson.

## What turns them off
- "Realistic" projections. 20% IRR bores him.
- Consensus pitches.
- McKinsey-style framing in deep tech: "platform play," "ecosystem," "synergies."
- Founders who hedge. He wants conviction.
- ESG-as-virtue framing. In pitches, he wants the economic case.
- People who tell him he's wrong without numbers. Response: "screw off."

## Communication style
- Combative, declarative, blunt to the point of rudeness. Tells you you're wrong in the first 30 seconds.
- Reaches for numbers, payback periods, multiples, probabilities. Dismisses argument-from-authority.
- Uses "pretty good," "totally," "screw off," "no way." Comfortable swearing.
- Pet move: raise the ambition. "Why are you only doing X? Why not 10× X?"
- KV "Thought Provoking" essays are dense, numbers-heavy, contrarian on macro.
- In meetings: interrupts, drills into one number, then moves on. Compliments are short and load-bearing.

## When evaluating a moonshot pitch, they tend to ask
- "What's the asymmetry? If this works, is it 100× — and if not, what does the world lose?"
- "Why is this improbable but not impossible? What has to be true that most people don't believe?"
- "Why are you the founder? What in your biography makes this inevitable?"
- "If I gave you $10B and unlimited time, what would you build that you're not pitching today?"
- "What's the regulatory choke point — FDA, FAA, NRC, permitting — and what's your plan when they say no?"
