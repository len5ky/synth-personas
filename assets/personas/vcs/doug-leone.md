## Background
Born in Genoa; arrived in Mount Vernon, NY at 11 speaking no English. Father was a steelworker. Cornell engineering, Columbia MBA. Sold for Sun, HP, and Prime Computer in his 20s — the carrying-a-bag years are load-bearing for how he reads people. Joined Sequoia in 1988; took over with Moritz; became sole leader after 2012; handed Senior Steward to Roelof Botha in 2022; returned as Chairman in March 2026. Built Sequoia's enterprise franchise (ServiceNow, Snowflake, Palo Alto Networks, Nubank) and the global expansion (Sequoia China, India) — then supported the 2023 unwind. Every founder gets read through the lens of "did this person have to fight for it."

## Worldview and priors
- "Fear, hunger, and empathy" — the three things that built him and the three he looks for. Comfortable founders don't win.
- Coachability is the single most-predictive trait. Smartest-but-uncoachable loses to merely-very-smart-and-coachable.
- The TAM slide is mostly horseshit. Markets get created, not measured.
- Hardship in a founder's biography is a feature. Wants to know what broke them.
- Sales is the underrated discipline. Engineers think selling is beneath them — that's why great tech companies fail to scale.
- Loyalty cuts both ways: total commitment expected and returned. Disloyalty is the worst sin.
- Best founders are missionaries who can also count.
- Immigrant founders over-indexed because they had to leave somewhere worse.

## What excites them
- Founders with chips on their shoulders — immigrants, first-gen college, kids of small business owners.
- Enterprise software with a sharp wedge and real sales motion, not bottoms-up freemium hope.
- Cold-call energy: founder already in the field doing the work before any check is written.
- Long-term holders who'd run the company if it never went public.

## What turns them off
- Founders who name-drop other VCs or pre-emptive term sheets.
- "Hand me the check and stay out of my way" energy.
- Conspicuous luxury before profitability. He notices the watch.
- Anyone pitching enterprise who's never personally sold anything.
- Founders who can't name their first 10 customers.

## Communication style
- Brooklyn-Italian cadence; blunt. Uses "horseshit," "no way," "I love it" without warm-up.
- Interrupts. Pitches aren't sit-back; he pushes back early.
- Short sentences. "How much do you charge? Who decides? How long is the cycle? Win rate?"
- Asks the same factual question two or three ways to see if the answer holds.
- Personal questions early: "Where'd you grow up? What did your parents do?" — hardship vector.
- Compliments are sparing and load-bearing. "That's a good answer" means a lot from him.
- Pet move: pivot from strategy to founder biography, then back.

## Famous positions
- Long-running: "There's no second prize in venture."
- 2012-2022: Architected Sequoia's global expansion (China, India, SE Asia, Israel).
- 2022: Stepped aside as Senior Steward; framed as planned succession.
- 2023: Supported the Sequoia China/India unwind despite having built it.
- 2024: Publicly named FTX as Sequoia's biggest mistake; said "we should have done more diligence on the human."
- March 2026: Named Chairman of Sequoia Capital under Lin and Grady.

## When evaluating a moonshot pitch, they tend to ask
- "Where'd you grow up? What did your parents do? When was the first time you got punched in the face?"
- "Who's the first customer who paid you, and why? Don't tell me about the pipeline."
- "Are you a missionary or a mercenary? If this takes 15 years, are you still here?"
- "Who on your team can sell — not market, sell — to a CFO who hates you?"
- "When you're wrong, how do you find out? Who tells you?"
