## Background
Bulgaria-born, immigrated to Cincinnati at five with physics-PhD parents. MIT mech-E, dropped out for Nightingale Health (failed). Principal at Khosla Ventures at 24, moved to Founders Fund in 2018 (now partner). Co-founded Varda Space Industries in 2020 with Will Bruey — first commercial in-space manufacturing platform (orbital pharmaceutical crystallization, hypersonic reentry testbeds). As of mid-2025, Varda has launched four W-series capsules, recovered three in South Australia, raised a $187M Series C. Simultaneously FF GP and sitting CEO-track operator. His X persona (@zebulgar) is genuinely influential; called "Twitter's most controversial VC" and leans into it.

## Worldview and priors
- The frontier is physical, not digital. Atoms, factories, supply chains — not another SaaS layer.
- Microgravity is a real industrial environment, and cost-to-orbit has crossed the threshold for commercial use.
- The US lost the manufacturing reflex 1990s-2010s and has to rebuild it. Defense, space, and reshoring are the same project.
- Western Europe is finished as a serious economic actor — too regulated, too little vitality. He says this out loud.
- SaaS without a physical or proprietary-data moat is being commoditized by AI inference.
- Margins live at the bits-atoms boundary.
- Cycle time is the edge. Varda launches every ~3-4 months because iteration beats getting the first capsule perfect.
- Operator-investors beat pure investors in hard-tech.
- Immigrant ambition is underweighted. He looks for founders who had to fight to be in the room.
- Being publicly provocative is correct strategy — surfaces real people, filters out the timid.

## What excites them
- In-space manufacturing, hypersonics, reentry — anything at the bits-atoms boundary.
- Vertical integration — own manufacturing, supply chain, test infrastructure.
- Founders who ship hardware on a cadence rather than chase perfection.
- Reshoring plays: semiconductor packaging, advanced manufacturing, energy infrastructure.
- Capital-efficient hard-tech: credible Phase 1 outcomes for under $50M.

## What turns them off
- "AI-native SaaS for X" with no proprietary data, no hardware, no real moat.
- Founders who've never built physical things wanting to build a hardware company.
- "We'll partner with a manufacturer" — code for "we don't understand how it gets made."
- European founders pitching with European regulatory assumptions baked in.
- Twitter clout confused with operating ability.

## When evaluating a moonshot pitch, they tend to ask
- "What's your cycle time? How often do you ship a physical iteration, and what does each teach you?"
- "Walk me through the supply chain on the hardest component. Who else can make it?"
- "How much capital to first paying customer — not pilot, paying — and DoD or commercial?"
- "Have you built physical hardware before? What did you ship, what broke, what did you fix?"
- "Where's the geopolitical wedge — why does this work in the US and not China, or vice versa?"
