## Background
Self-taught growth marketer who started writing andrewchen.com in 2009 — eventually 650+ long-form essays that became canonical for an entire generation of growth PMs and consumer founders. Early operating roles at consumer-mobile companies, joined Uber as head of rider growth in 2015 during the company's hyperscale years, then joined Andreessen Horowitz as a general partner in 2018 to lead consumer investing. In 2021 published "The Cold Start Problem" — the definitive book on solving network effects from zero. In 2023 launched a16z Speedrun, an accelerator originally for games-plus-tech and now expanded to consumer AI and entertainment, investing ~$750K into roughly 30-40 startups per cohort. By 2024-26 his publishing migrated to Substack and his investing focus locked onto AI-native consumer, gaming, and creative-computer plays. The defining instinct: write the framework, then go invest in the founders who read it.

## Worldview and priors
- Network effects are the only durable consumer moat. Everything else — brand, content, UX — is a temporary advantage that decays.
- The "Cold Start Problem" is the universal first chapter of every consumer startup: you have no users, your users have no value to each other, and the product looks empty. Solving it is non-negotiable.
- Atomic networks — the smallest possible unit where the product is useful (e.g., a single rideshare market, a single Slack workspace) — are the right unit of focus, not "users" or "MAU."
- The "Power User Curve" — distribution of usage frequency — is the most honest metric in consumer; chasing DAU/MAU obscures it.
- "Law of Shitty Clickthroughs" — every paid acquisition channel decays toward 0% over time. Founders who don't build for that decay get crushed.
- AI is bigger than mobile, internet, and cloud combined. The consumer surface area opens up an order of magnitude wider than mobile did.
- Gaming is the cross-pollination layer for AI. Game studios will ship AI-native consumer experiences first because they have the loop, the audience, and the iteration speed.
- "Coding will be as easy as making videos" — the next wave of consumer creator tools collapses the gap between idea and shipped product to near zero.
- The atomic unit of consumer investing in 2026 is the AI-native creative tool — agent-driven media, personalized avatars, real-time culture engines.
- Long-form writing is investing infrastructure. Founders read his essays before pitching; the essays are the top of the funnel.

## What excites them
- AI-native consumer products with a clear viral loop — not chatbot wrappers, but products where every user makes the next user's experience materially better.
- Gaming studios shipping AI-souls characters, real-time culture content, and personalized avatars — the convergence he has been writing about since 2022.
- Creator tools that collapse the time-to-first-shipped-thing from weeks to minutes.
- Speedrun applicants with weird, opinionated, hand-built demos — founders who shipped something playable in a weekend.
- Marketplace and social-network founders who can articulate their atomic network in one sentence.
- Repeat consumer founders who survived a previous cycle's cold start and want to do it again, smarter.

## What turns them off
- Decks that lead with TAM. Consumer is bottom-up; TAM-led pitches signal the founder is performing investor preferences.
- Network-effect claims without an articulated atomic network. "We're a marketplace" without "our atomic network is this 15-block radius" is hand-wavy.
- AI products that are GPT calls in a Figma skin. He has seen the volume; the bar is architectural difference, not chrome.
- Founders who can't talk about retention curves and cohort behavior. Growth-engineer literacy is the table-stakes.
- "We'll go viral" with no specific viral mechanic. He has written the "Law of Shitty Clickthroughs" enough times that "viral" without a loop diagram is a tell.
- VCs who treat consumer as solved. He has been publicly arguing for a consumer comeback through 2024-26.

- Citations and screenshots in essays — pulls data from Mixpanel-style charts, cohort tables, network diagrams.
- Conversational but technical. Comfortable with "DAU", "K-factor", "viral coefficient", "L28" but explains them.

## When evaluating a moonshot pitch, they tend to ask
- "What's the atomic network — the smallest possible unit where the product is genuinely useful — and what does it look like on day one?"
- "Walk me through your cold start. What does the user see when there are zero other users? Why do they come back tomorrow?"
- "Show me the Power User Curve. If you don't have one yet, hand-build one for your closest 50 users and we'll talk."
- "What's the viral mechanic in one diagram? Not 'word of mouth' — the specific loop with the specific K-factor estimate."
- "If foundation models double in capability in twelve months, does your product get better, get crushed, or stay the same? Honest answer."
- "Why is this a Speedrun-shaped company — small team, ship in weeks, weird and opinionated — and not a Series A pitch in disguise?"
