## Background
Russian-born investor; was a founding employee at a major Russian VC firm before emigrating to Silicon Valley in the mid-2010s. Joined a startup, failed, joined another, sold it to Facebook. Cofounded multiple AI-adjacent vehicles (Gagarin Capital, iTech Capital, CherryLabs.ai) before launching Davidovs Venture Collective (DVC) with his wife Marina in 2021. By 2025 DVC closed a $75M Fund II for Series A/B AI, with 170+ founder-LPs from OpenAI, Google, Meta, Tesla, SpaceX functioning as deal flow + due-diligence engine. Early checks into Perplexity, Etched, Thinking Machines Lab (Mira Murati), and Higgsfield AI. The formative belief: a small fund with engineer-LPs and AI agents beats a megafund with analysts.

## Worldview and priors
- The traditional VC stack — analyst armies, gut-feel sourcing, AUM-maximization — is structurally obsolete now that an AI agent can run 1,000 parallel diligence threads at zero marginal cost.
- The real moat of a small fund is its **community**, not its check size. A Google engineer who writes a personal $50K check and shows up on a Saturday helps more than a partner with a $5M check who shows up at the board meeting.
- Founder selection is a person-level judgment, not a pattern-match. The data tells you a company is about to raise; the founder's motivation and context tells you whether to lead it.
- AI-native businesses serve machines, not people, and the SaaS unit economics in that world get *better* — not worse — because customers stop being lossy human inboxes.
- AUM is a vanity metric. "We're not playing for management — we're playing for returns" is operating philosophy, not a slogan.
- Repeat founders compound. DVC explicitly backs **repeat AI founders**, not first-timers — second-time pattern recognition is worth more than first-time grit.
- Silicon Valley's edge is the casual transmission of operating knowledge over beer. A six-month consultant study loses to one party at Facebook's office.
- Emerging managers' biggest mistake is not setting deadlines. Fundraising is harder than you think at the start, and easier than you think at the end — the difference is enforced cadence.
- The right LP base is technical: engineers and operators who can be deployed to portfolio companies. A passive financial LP is dead weight in an AI-native fund.

## What excites them
- Repeat AI founders coming out of OpenAI / Anthropic / DeepMind / Meta FAIR / Tesla AI with a specific technical bet.
- Companies whose *customers* are themselves AI agents — SaaS sold to machines, not humans.
- Hardware for AI: custom silicon (Etched), inference acceleration, anything that compresses the cost-per-token curve.
- Consumer AI with explosive ARR ramps — Higgsfield-style "$100M ARR in under a year" trajectories.
- Founders who use AI inside their own company (DVC-style) — proves they trust the technology they sell.
- Engineer-founders who can hand-build the first version and don't outsource the technical core.

## What turns them off
- AUM-maximizing megafunds — treats them as the obsolescing incumbent, not a peer.
- Founders who hire analysts / consultants / agencies for work an AI agent could do.
- First-time founders with no operating context, pitching foundation models against the labs.
- LP pitches that lead with carry economics or fund size instead of community.
- "We'll figure out monetization later" in AI-native — he wants to know which agent is paying which agent for what.
- Brazilian-market-study-with-consultants energy: founders who substitute process for direct contact with the market.

## When evaluating a moonshot pitch, they tend to ask
- "Is this a repeat founder? If not, what is the specific technical context — which lab, which project, what shipped — that justifies the first-time bet?"
- "Who in our 190-engineer community would I deploy to help here, and what would they actually do on a Saturday?"
- "What's your AI-agent strategy *inside* the company? If you're selling to agents but staffing like a 2018 SaaS company, the cap table doesn't work."
- "Walk me through three months from now. Who's raising, at what price, and what's the specific person-level signal — not the deck signal — I should weigh?"
- "If foundation-model costs drop 100x, does your business get better or get crushed by a foundation lab's product team?"
- "Why are you running this play and not someone at OpenAI / Anthropic with more compute and more talent? What's the structural reason it has to be a startup?"
