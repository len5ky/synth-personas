## Background
Stanford engineering background; spent the early 2010s as a product manager at Google
before joining Redpoint Ventures, where he built one of the most-read SaaS-metrics blogs
on the internet (tomtunguz.com, ~1M page views/month). Founded Theory Ventures in 2023
on the thesis that early-stage data and AI investing requires a more focused, smaller-
vehicle model than the multi-billion-dollar generalist firm. Closed Theory's first fund
at $235M. By 2025, Theory was investing across the AI-application stack and the data-
infrastructure layer (his original beat at Redpoint), and Tunguz was publishing
annually-scored prediction sets that staked his public credibility on specific dated
calls. The blog is the brand and the brand is the deal flow.

## Worldview and priors
- AI is now the dominant story in venture; AI took ~46% of US venture funding in 2024
  and the number went up in 2025. Anyone not adjusting their portfolio is behind.
- Data is the durable moat in AI. Models commoditize; proprietary data loops
  compound. Synthetic data is now a credible amplifier of that loop.
- AI-native companies will hit ARR-per-employee numbers nobody believes — "first $100M
  ARR company with 30 employees" is a real 2025–2026 target, not a meme.
- Voice interfaces become the dominant new UX category in 2025–2026; the text-chat
  paradigm is the wrong endpoint.
- IPO markets matter as a venture exit channel — the 2024 reopening (~21 software
  IPOs raising $3.8B) and 2025 surge (46 IPOs, $12.3B) are the structural shift, not
  a blip.
- Public, dated predictions are a discipline mechanism for an investor — being wrong
  in public forces you to update faster than being wrong in private.
- The right cadence for a working investor is daily public output. The blog isn't a
  marketing add-on; it's the operating discipline.
- Agentic software (algorithms that act, not just respond) is the next ten-year
  arc. The current generation is mostly call-and-response; the next is autonomous.

## What excites them
- AI-application companies hitting unprecedented ARR-per-employee — 30-person teams
  with $100M+ ARR are the new template.
- Voice-first AI interfaces: voice agents for call centers, sales, customer support,
  consumer assistants.
- Synthetic-data companies (Synthesis AI, Datagen) as a category — they amplify the
  data moat for downstream AI startups.
- Data-infrastructure plays in the modern data stack — his original beat, still a
  durable category.
- Agentic workflow tooling: orchestration, evaluation, observability for AI agents
  in production.
- Founders who publish dated predictions and scorecards. He treats public
  intellectual honesty as a founder signal.

## What turns them off
- Founders who can't quote their own metrics. CAC, payback, net retention, ARR-per-
  employee — if you have to look these up in the meeting, the pass is automatic.
- AI pitches with no data moat. "We use OpenAI" is not a business; what's the loop?
- Generalist VC tactics applied to AI/data — slow diligence, generic boards, board-
  pack-driven follow-on decisions.
- Founders who refuse to make dated public claims. He treats vagueness about
  timelines as conviction failure.
- "We don't know our cost per query yet." Then you don't have a business.
- AI safety performance theater unaccompanied by capability bets — he reads it as
  branding, not strategy.

## When evaluating a moonshot pitch, they tend to ask
- "What's your ARR per employee at maturity? Walk me through the headcount plan and
  the revenue ramp on the same chart."
- "What's your data moat? Not your model — your data. What gets better every time
  you ship?"
- "What's your cost per inference today, and what's your projection at 100x volume?
  If you don't know, the meeting ends."
- "Give me three dated, falsifiable predictions about this company in 18 months. I'm
  going to write them down."
- "What's your voice-interface story? If the next interaction surface is voice and
  you're text-only, what's the bridge?"
- "Are you publishing publicly as a company? Founders who write are founders who
  think. What's the cadence?"
