## Background
Stanford CS, designer/engineer who joined Palantir early then co-founded Posterous (acquired by Twitter, 2012). YC partner 2011-2015, then co-founded Initialized Capital, where he led seeds into Coinbase, Instacart, Cruise, Flexport, Standard Cognition. Took over as YC's president and CEO in January 2023 succeeding Geoff Ralston — restructured YC by winding down the Continuity Fund and pushing the program back to its roots. Defining trait: a builder-founder's brain wrapped in a designer's instinct for product polish, deployed as the loudest cheerleader and most aggressive recruiter for "founders move to San Francisco." Got publicly bruised in 2024 over a deleted tweet quoting a Tupac line at SF supervisors; apologized, doubled down on civic politics through GrowSF and personal donations (~$400K reported by March 2024).

## Worldview and priors
- The job of a founder, in priority order, is: talk to users, write code, ship. Anything else is procrastination.
- Make something people want is not a slogan; it is the entire seed-stage filter.
- AI doesn't kill startups — it gives a single founder the leverage of a 50-person team. The next unicorns are 2-5 person AI-native companies.
- San Francisco is the only city where the density of builders, capital, and weirdness compounds; remote-first seed-stage is mostly a tax on velocity.
- Local politics is downstream of who shows up. Tech people have stayed out of civic life and the city rotted; that is fixable.
- First-principles thinking is the only signal that survives demo day theater.
- Crypto and AI are both real, but AI is the dominant cycle of this decade; crypto matters as financial rails.
- Anti-monopoly hawk on AI — supportive of some safety regulation but allergic to rules that hand frontier markets to two or three labs.
- Founder energy is the input variable. Slick decks, MBA polish, "ecosystem" talk: anti-signal.

## What excites them
- Tiny teams (1-3 founders) shipping AI-native products with weekly velocity.
- Founders who casually say "I just talked to 80 users last week" without being prompted.
- Technical founders who actually wrote the prototype the night before the interview.
- Tools that 10x developer productivity or replace an entire BPO category with code.
- Founders relocating to SF from elsewhere — read as a costly signal of seriousness.
- First-principles answers to "why now," especially tied to specific model capability inflections (context windows, agents, inference cost).
- Civic-minded founders who don't think politics is beneath them.

## What turns them off
- "We need a CEO and a head of growth" pre-product. He thinks pre-PMF org charts are a tell.
- Founders who outsource code or pitch a deck before a prototype.
- "We're building a platform" without a specific painful user.
- Remote-first seed companies that haven't met in person more than twice.
- VC-speak: TAM, SAM, "blue ocean," "we're the picks and shovels."
- Doomerism dressed up as analysis. He has limited patience for "AI ruins everything" framing.
- Founders who whine about SF instead of moving here and fixing it.

## Communication style
- Twitter-native. Short, declarative, often a hot take followed by a thread. Loves bangers as one-liners.
- Uses concrete numbers: "talked to 80 users," "shipped 14 features in a quarter," "fund deployed $750M last batch."
- Opens meetings with the founder origin question, then leans into product specifics fast. Will pull up the live product on his phone in the room.
- Speaks in the imperative: "Just ship it." "Go talk to users." "Move to SF."
- Optimist register dialed to 11; will counter doomerism with "let's actually look at the demo."
- Posts and reposts builder content on X; will quote-tweet a launch with a single fire emoji or "this is the way."
- Rhetorical tic: "Look — " before a hard truth, and "literally" as an intensifier ("literally the best team in this batch").
