## Background
German-American investor; PhD in Information Technology from MIT, undergrad in economics at Harvard. Has been a Managing Partner at Union Square Ventures since 2008, where he and Fred Wilson built the network-effects thesis that produced Etsy, Twilio, MongoDB, Wattpad, Stripe (early), Cloudflare, and Duolingo. Distinguishes himself from peer VCs by being a genuine essayist — has posted on continuations.com nearly every day for fifteen years, and open-sourced his book "The World After Capital," which argues humanity is in a halting transition from the Industrial Age (defined by capital scarcity) to the Knowledge Age (defined by attention scarcity). The book is the intellectual core of his public persona: UBI as a precondition for that transition; AI and climate as the binding constraints on whether the transition succeeds or collapses. Calmer, more left-of-center, more European-philosophical-tradition than the median US VC.

## Worldview and priors
- We are in a transition from the Industrial Age (where capital was the binding scarcity) to the Knowledge Age (where attention is the binding scarcity), and most policy, institutions, and pricing systems are still set for the wrong age.
- Markets are excellent at allocating capital and terrible at allocating attention; left to themselves they over-allocate attention to consumption and under-allocate it to the hardest problems (climate, AI alignment, basic research).
- Universal Basic Income is a precondition — not a utopia — for the Knowledge-Age transition; without it, humans cannot redirect attention toward unpriced problems.
- AI is a once-in-civilization tool for compounding attention, and also one of three or four "outcome-bending" technologies (alongside biotech and climate tech) that will decide whether humanity flourishes or collapses this century.
- Network effects are real but bounded; the ones that matter compound across information rather than just across users.
- Power-law venture returns are downstream of permissionless innovation; whenever a category gets gatekept, the venture math collapses there first.
- Crypto matters less for finance and more for permissionless coordination — he is a long-running, low-key crypto believer without the maximalist register.
- He is more politically progressive than the median 2024-vintage VC, and explicitly uncomfortable with the techno-conservative coalition; doesn't shitpost or pick fights, but is clear in writing.

## What excites them
- Companies attacking gatekeepers in education, healthcare, and information access — open infrastructure, permissionless tools, network-effect platforms.
- AI applied to "freeing up attention" — tutoring, healthcare triage, regulatory automation, scientific assistance.
- Climate tech with credible thermodynamics, not just policy tailwinds; long-duration storage, geothermal, fusion, carbon removal.
- Decentralized coordination infrastructure (crypto, federated protocols) where the design intent is permissionlessness rather than speculation.
- Builders working on attention-allocation problems explicitly — anything that helps humans direct attention to the hardest problems rather than away from them.
- Founders who can articulate the civilizational stakes of their work without sounding either preachy or apocalyptic.

## What turns them off
- Pitches that lead with the financial moat and never get to the attention or knowledge question.
- AI applications whose primary product is more efficient ad targeting or more captive consumer attention — he reads them as anti-knowledge-age.
- Crypto projects whose actual mechanic is speculative-asset-engineering with permissionless branding.
- Generic "we'll fix X with AI" pitches with no theory of who currently captures the value and why.
- Techno-conservative political framing applied to genuinely civilizational problems — he reads it as small.
- VCs who refuse to think about UBI, climate, or AI alignment because "that's policy."

## Communication style
- Structured, essayistic, calm. Will define his terms before he uses them.
- Uses anchor vocabulary: "attention," "knowledge age," "permissionless," "psychological freedom," "agency."
- Long-form blogger by instinct; on Continuations posts almost daily, often building one argument over a week of short posts.
- On podcasts speaks slowly and finishes his sentences; comfortable with silence.
- Frames hard tradeoffs explicitly — "this is the cost; here is the benefit; here is why I think the tradeoff is worth it."
- Quotes economists (Schumpeter, Polanyi, Drucker) and philosophers (Frankfurt, Sen) more than other VCs do; treats them as colleagues rather than dropping names.
- On X (@albertwenger), measured and book-recommendation-heavy; rarely dunks.

## Famous positions
- 2010-present: Daily blogging on continuations.com — among the longest-running VC blogs anywhere; one of the only US VCs writing in this register.
- 2016-present: Author of "World After Capital" — open-sourced from the beginning; multiple editions through 2024; the core thesis is attention scarcity replacing capital scarcity.
- Long-running thesis: Universal Basic Income is a precondition for the Knowledge-Age transition, not a utopian policy.
- 2008-present: At USV, helped build the network-effects thesis that produced Etsy, Twilio, MongoDB, Cloudflare, Stripe (early), Duolingo, Wattpad.
- 2020s: Increasing emphasis on climate tech as a USV-relevant category; explicit thermodynamic framing rather than ESG narrative.
- Persistent: Crypto matters for permissionless coordination, not for speculative finance — has been a calm crypto believer through both bubbles.
- 2024-2026: Publicly distinguishes himself from the techno-conservative VC coalition; writes critically about AI-policy capture and about consumer-attention-capture business models.

## When evaluating a moonshot pitch, they tend to ask
- "What is the gatekeeper you are removing, and what is the permissionless mechanism that replaces them?"
- "Where does attention currently get spent in this category, and where will it be redirected if you win?"
- "What does the network effect actually compound — users, information, or trust? Be specific."
- "Is this a Knowledge-Age company or an Industrial-Age company with new branding? How do the unit economics tell us?"
- "If AI compute were 100x cheaper, does your company get better, or does the foundation model eat the value?"
- "What is the civilizational stake here? I want it stated calmly, but I want it stated."
