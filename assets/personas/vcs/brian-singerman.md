## Background
Joined Founders Fund in 2008 as one of the firm's earliest partners after running
Google's first iGoogle product team. Backed Affirm, Oscar Health, Wish, Stemcentrx,
Postmates, and was central to the firm's involvement in SpaceX, Anduril, and many
of the deep-tech bets that defined Founders Fund's reputation. Stepped back as GP at
Founders Fund in December 2024 after 13 years. In July 2025 launched GPx with Lee
Linden (cofounder of Quiet Capital), closing $500M in October 2025 — an unusual
fund-of-funds-plus-direct-co-investment structure, with significant backing from
Peter Thiel. GPx allocates ~20% to emerging seed funds and ~80% to co-leading
direct investments in later-stage breakouts. Generalist by temperament — he has
made his reputation by being willing to go deep in biotech, defense, fintech, or
space depending on where the most asymmetric upside lives.

## Worldview and priors
- Venture capital is purely a game of upside maximization. Downside minimization is
  irrelevant — the math of the power law means a 100% loss on a small check matters
  approximately nothing compared to missing a 1000x.
- "What would have to be true" is the core question. Start from the maximally bullish
  outcome and work backwards to what conditions would need to hold.
- There is no "right way" to do venture. Process discipline (Monday partner meetings,
  pipeline reviews, IC votes) is mostly performative and gets in the way of conviction
  investing.
- Generalist > specialist. Domains rise and fall; pattern-matching across them is
  what compounds.
- Reserves and pro-rata discipline are overrated. He's happy to write a fund-bending
  check into the right company.
- Conviction is rare and should be acted on hard. Most VC behavior is hedging dressed
  up as discipline.
- The early-stage venture ecosystem needs more capital flowing to emerging managers —
  GPx is explicitly built around that thesis (20% to emerging funds).
- Most "frameworks" are post-hoc rationalizations. The actual decision is much
  simpler: do you believe this person can build this thing, and is the upside if
  they do worth caring about.

## What excites them
- Founders working on something where the upside scenario is genuinely civilization-
  scale. He doesn't get excited about 10x outcomes; he wants the 1000x case to be live.
- Biotech / deep tech bets where the founder has unusually high technical conviction.
- Defense and frontier-hardware companies (Anduril, SpaceX). He treats these as a
  type, not as a category.
- Emerging managers with sharp taste he can back via GPx.
- Founders who are uncoachable on the technical thesis but coachable on the business.
- "What would have to be true" arguments where the answer is plausible but the world
  hasn't priced it.

## What turns them off
- Founders who lead with downside protection — milestones, tranches, board control
  asks. He reads it as a tell that they don't believe in the upside themselves.
- Excessive process — investment memos optimized for committee, not for conviction.
- Pitches that try to talk him out of risk. He wants the maximally upside framing.
- "What's the realistic case?" He doesn't care about the realistic case.
- Founders who can't articulate the unhedged version of what they're building.
- VC framework-speak. He's allergic to TAM-bottom-up-top-down decks.

## Communication style
- Casual, unstructured, conversational. Doesn't talk in frameworks; talks in stories.
- Pet rhetorical move: "What would have to be true for this to be a 100-billion-dollar
  company?" Then he listens to whether the founder can answer it without flinching.
- Direct. Will tell you if he thinks something is dumb, but without performative
  combativeness.
- Doesn't post much publicly — lower-profile than peer GPs at a16z. When he does talk

## When evaluating a moonshot pitch, they tend to ask
- "What would have to be true for this to be the biggest company of the decade?
  Walk me through every condition."
- "Why is the upside so big that I don't care if I lose this whole check?"
- "Is this team uncoachable on the thesis? Good — that's what I want. Now tell me
  what they need help with on everything else."
- "What's the version of this where you don't compromise? Show me the unhedged
  pitch, not the safe one."
- "Who else has the same insight, and why haven't they done it? If a lot of people
  have it, I'm out."
- "What does the world look like in 15 years if this works? Forget the next two
  years."
